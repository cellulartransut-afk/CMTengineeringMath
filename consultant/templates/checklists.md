# Checklists

## 1. Vet a new client before you quote

- [ ] Who owns the property? (Ask. For new builds, ask to see the conveyance or survey.)
- [ ] Who decides and who pays? Same person?
- [ ] Budget stated, and realistic for the scope?
- [ ] How did they find you? Referral beats cold.
- [ ] Have they fired other contractors on this job? Ask why. One is normal; two is a warning.
- [ ] Will they pay a 30% to 40% deposit? If not, walk.
- [ ] Do they need a permit and drawings, and do they have them?
- [ ] Timeline realistic?
- [ ] Gut check: if they argue price hard before you start, they argue payment harder at the end.

## 2. Vet a new subcontractor

- [ ] Visit a job they are working on now. Look at the quality and the site.
- [ ] Two references from contractors, called.
- [ ] Business licence, NIB letter of good standing, public liability certificate.
- [ ] Trade licence (electrical, plumbing).
- [ ] Crew legally allowed to work in The Bahamas.
- [ ] Own tools and transport.
- [ ] Quote in writing for a defined scope.
- [ ] Will they sign your subcontract? If not, don't use them.
- [ ] Start them on a small job first.

## 3. Before you send a quote

- [ ] Site visited and photographed
- [ ] Every work item in sequence listed
- [ ] Quantities with math shown
- [ ] Materials priced landed and delivered, quotes dated
- [ ] Sub quotes in writing
- [ ] General conditions added
- [ ] Family Island logistics priced (if applicable)
- [ ] Contingency added
- [ ] Margin checked (not markup confused for margin)
- [ ] Exclusions listed
- [ ] Payment schedule keeps your cash positive
- [ ] Validity date matches supplier quote validity
- [ ] Cost per sq ft or per room compared with past jobs

## 4. Before any concrete pour

- [ ] Inspection passed (yours and Building Control where required)
- [ ] Rebar size, spacing, laps, cover on chairs checked against the drawing
- [ ] Straps, anchors, sleeves, conduits set
- [ ] Formwork braced and clean, oiled
- [ ] Concrete ordered: strength, slump, quantity + 5% to 10%, time
- [ ] Pump or access confirmed
- [ ] Vibrator, tools, labour, water for curing, cover in case of rain
- [ ] Weather checked; in heat, pour early
- [ ] Cylinders arranged if required
- [ ] Rule told to the crew and driver: no water added without your OK

## 5. Hurricane: 72 hours out

- [ ] Loose materials and debris secured or removed
- [ ] Skip bin emptied or removed
- [ ] Scaffolding lowered or tied down
- [ ] Open roofs covered and sheathing fastened
- [ ] Shutters installed
- [ ] Signs and banners removed
- [ ] Generator and vehicles fuelled
- [ ] Tools and equipment removed or locked
- [ ] Whole site photographed
- [ ] Documents backed up
- [ ] Client and subs notified; crew released in time

## 6. Closeout

- [ ] Punch list walked and signed
- [ ] Final clean
- [ ] Final inspections, certificate of occupancy
- [ ] Warranties, manuals, product list, paint colours handed over
- [ ] Photos of finished work and inside walls (before closing) filed
- [ ] Final invoice; retention date agreed
- [ ] Subs paid; sub retention release dates set
- [ ] Testimonial requested
- [ ] Job logged in `my-business/jobs.md` with real cost and lessons

## 7. Monthly business check

- [ ] Bank reconciled
- [ ] VAT, NIB and business licence reserves in the tax account
- [ ] NIB return filed by the 15th (if employer)
- [ ] VAT return filed (if registered); rolling 12-month turnover checked against B$100,000
- [ ] Money owed to you listed; late payers chased
- [ ] Profit per job vs estimate reviewed
- [ ] Overhead vs budget
- [ ] Backlog: how many weeks of signed work?
- [ ] Sub bench updated
- [ ] One thing to improve next month
