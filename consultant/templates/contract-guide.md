# Contract Guide

Rev 1 · 2026-11-04

> **Template note.** For you, the contractor. Read it before you use any contract in this folder. It tells you which document fits which job, what you never remove, what you can give away, how to get the contract signed, and when to update the wording. Have a Bahamian attorney review this template once before first use, together with the rest of the set.

## The one rule

The Bahamas has no mechanics' lien law. When a client won't pay, you cannot put a lien on the property. The contract is your only security. The deposit, stage payments, interest, the right to stop work and title to materials are how you get paid.

A contract nobody signs protects nobody. That is why this set is short, plain and fair. A client who can read it in ten minutes signs it. A client who needs an attorney to read it goes to the next contractor.

## 1. Which document to use

| Situation | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing any job, before the contract | `quote-proposal.md` | Scope, exclusions, price and payment agreed before the contract |
| Small job, clear scope, under about B$10,000 | `short-form-job-agreement.md` | Two pages. Signed in ten minutes. Every protection in short sentences. |
| Job of about B$10,000 to B$250,000, or any job with drawings, a permit or structural work | `owner-contract-small-works.md` | Full clauses, scope and exclusion schedules, practical completion, retention |
| Repair where the problem can't be seen until it is opened up (leaks, rot, termites, water damage) | `time-and-materials-repair-agreement.md` | Client pays actual cost up to a cap they control. You never gamble a fixed price on what you can't see. |
| Recurring work for landlords, property managers, gated communities, short-term rental owners (many living abroad) | `maintenance-service-agreement.md` | Monthly fee in advance. Work Orders for everything else. Recurring income. |
| Over about B$250,000, or the client's architect or engineer administers the job | JCT (for example the Minor Works or Intermediate forms) or FIDIC (for example the Short Form), reviewed by a Bahamian attorney | Too much risk for a home-made form. The attorney picks the form and writes the amendments. |
| Every subcontractor, every job | `subcontract-agreement.md` | Flows your contract down to the sub. Documents before start. Retention. |
| Every change to scope, price or time | `change-order.md` | No signed change order, no extra work |
| Every invoice | `payment-application.md` | Bills by stage, shows what is owed and when |
| Something goes wrong or must be on record | `notice-letters.md` | Twelve ready letters with the clause each one uses |
| Every payment in or out, and handover | `receipts-and-releases.md` | Receipts, sub releases, completion certificate, warranty certificate |
| Client sends you their own contract | Read it against the red flags in `knowledge/03-contracts.md`. Send it to your attorney if it is over about B$[25,000] or has delay penalties, indemnities or retention. | Their form was written for them |

**Pick by risk, not only by price.** A B$7,000 job with a permit, drawings or structural work goes on the small works agreement. The price bands are a guide.

**Two-step jobs.** When you can't see the problem, open up on the T&M agreement with a cap. Once you can see it, price the rebuild as a fixed price on the short form or small works agreement. You don't gamble, and the client doesn't sign an open cheque.

**Maintenance clients who want a project.** Anything over the limit in clause 5.3 of the maintenance agreement goes on its own agreement.

### The paper trail on one job

| Step | Document |
|---|---|
| 1. Price agreed | `quote-proposal.md`, signed |

## 2. Never remove

These clauses are the whole point of the contract. If a client asks you to delete one, explain why it is there (section 4). If they still insist, walk away. The jobs you don't take can't hurt you.

| Clause | Small Works | Short-Form | T&M Repair | Maintenance | Why it stays |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deposit | 4.1 | Page 1 table, term 2 | 4 | 3.1 (first month on signing; fees in advance) | Buys the materials and mobilisation, so you never finance the client's job |
| Payment schedule | 4.1 | Page 1 table | 7.1, 7.2 | 3 | Money in matches or leads money out at every stage |
| Late payment interest and right to suspend | 4.2, 4.3 | 3, 4 | 7.3, 7.4 | 14.1, 14.2 | With no lien law, stopping work is your only real pressure. Interest makes late payment cost the client, not you. |
| Title to materials until paid | 4.4 | 5 | 7.5 | 14.4 | Lets you take back materials not yet built in, instead of gifting them |
| No change without a signed change order | 7 | 6 | 6 (and the cap, 3) | 5, 6 | Scope you didn't write down is scope you do for free |
| Unforeseen conditions | 8 | 7 | 8 | 10 | Hidden rot, termites and bad old wiring are the most common way a fixed price on a Bahamian renovation goes wrong |
| Time extensions for weather, hurricanes, owner delays, shipping | 5.3 | 8 | 9.2 | 4.3, 9.5 | You don't control storms, the mailboat, customs, suppliers, Building Control or the client's decisions |
| Warranty limits | 12 | 11 | 12 | 10.2, 11 | 12 months, workmanship only, clear exclusions. Without limits the warranty never ends. |
| Liability cap | 17.6 | 12 | 13 | 12 | One leak claim for loss of rent or contents can cost more than ten jobs' profit |
| Governing law and disputes | 16, 17.1 | 16, 17 | 16 | 16 | Bahamian law, talk first, then a set route. Overseas clients can't drag you into a foreign court. |

Also keep: costs of recovery (Small Works 4.5), owner duties (6), notices by email and WhatsApp (17.3) and entire agreement (17.2).

**Liability cap for the small works agreement.** Clause 17.6 has now been added to `owner-contract-small-works.md` with this wording. Ask your attorney to confirm it at the first review:

> **17.6 Limit of liability.** The Contractor's total liability under or in connection with this Agreement is limited to [the Contract Price / B$[ ]]. The Contractor is not liable for loss of rent, profit or use, or other indirect loss. This does not limit liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, or for fraud.

## 3. Negotiable: what you can give, and what you get back

Never give something for nothing. Every concession buys you something back. Write every agreed change as a special condition: in the Special Conditions box on the short form, or as a numbered "Special Conditions" list signed with the small works agreement. Never edit the core wording for one client.

| Client asks for | You can give | Ask for in return | The line you don't cross |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lower deposit | A lower deposit, but only if the client buys the main materials or pays the supplier direct | Supplier paid before the order is placed; deliveries checked by you; warranty covers installation only on client-bought items | The deposit must still cover anything you would otherwise pay for out of your own pocket |
| Pay everything at the end | Nothing. Offer more, smaller stages instead (weekly or per milestone). | | Don't. Walk. |
| Longer payment terms (14 or 30 days) | Up to [14] days for businesses, property managers and repeat clients who pay on time | Payment by bank transfer; more frequent stages; interest and suspension stay | Keep the right to suspend. For government, price the wait. |
| Remove late interest | A lower rate, or a [7]-day grace period before interest starts | The right to suspend stays exactly as written | Don't delete the clause |
| Retention or holdback | Up to 5% until the punch list is done, or up to 10% until the end of the defects period, with a fixed release date | Deposit and stage payments unchanged; release on a date, not "when satisfied" | No retention over 10%. No retention without a release date. |
| Penalty for finishing late | Delay damages at a weekly amount tied to the client's real loss, capped at 5% of the price, as their only remedy for delay | All the time extensions stay; client decisions and payments on time; [a bonus for finishing early] | Never uncapped. Never without the extension clause. |
| Fixed price on work you can't see | Fixed price for the visible work, plus T&M with a cap (or a stated allowance) for the hidden part | Hidden conditions clause stays | Never a fixed price for unknowns |
| Longer warranty | 2 years on named items, if the manufacturer and the sub back it | A higher price; the client does the maintenance listed in the warranty | Never cover storms, misuse, wear or other people's work |
| Higher liability cap | Up to your public liability insurance limit for property damage | Proof the client insures the building and contents | Never unlimited. Never loss of rent or other indirect loss. |
| Owner supplies materials | Fine | Delivery dates; a handling fee; owner carries lateness and defects; you warrant installation only | Don't warrant what you didn't buy |
| Remove price escalation (Small Works 9) | Fine on short jobs where your supplier quotes are locked | Quote valid only [30] days; materials ordered on the deposit | Keep it on long jobs with steel, lumber, roofing or freight |
| Drop the 10% lost-overhead payment on termination (Small Works 15.2) | Fine on small jobs | Payment for work done, materials ordered and demobilisation stays | |
| Arbitration instead of courts, or the reverse | Either is fine. Courts usually suit small sums. | | Keep Bahamian law and the "talk first" step |
| Approve changes by WhatsApp | Fine, if your message states the change, price and time and they reply "approved" | Screenshot filed; a signed Change Order at the next visit for anything over the short-form limit | No verbal changes |
| Maintenance: shorter term or no auto-renewal | Month-to-month at a higher fee, or keep 12 months with the [60]-day early exit (clause 15.2) | Higher fee for month-to-month | Fees stay in advance |
| Use their contract instead of yours | Read every clause against the red flags | Attach your scope, exclusions and payment schedule as a schedule that governs if there is a conflict | Never sign amendments you haven't read |

## 4. How to present it so it gets signed

**Before the meeting**
1. Fill every blank. No "we'll fill that in later".
2. Send the PDF a day ahead by WhatsApp or email: "Here's the agreement for your [job]. I'll walk you through it in two minutes when we meet."
3. Bring two printed copies and a pen. Sit down at a table, not on the tailgate with the crew waiting.
4. Never rush anyone. Tell them they can take it home tonight.

**The two-minute walk-through.** Say it in your own words, but hit every point.

> "Before you sign, let me take two minutes to walk you through this. It protects both of us. You know exactly what you're getting and what it costs. I know I get paid for the work I do. Nothing in here is a trick. If anything doesn't sit right, tell me now.
>
> **What we're doing.** [Read the scope lines.] That's the job. If it isn't on this list, it isn't in the price.
>
> **What's not included.** [Point to the exclusions.] Most arguments start here, so I list them up front. The big one: if we open up and find rot or termites, I stop, send you photos and give you a price before we fix it. You decide.
>
> **How payment works.** [Point to the table.] The deposit buys your materials and books the crew. After that you pay as each stage is done, so you're never far ahead of the work, and I'm never paying for your job out of my own pocket. If a payment runs late, interest runs and I can stop work after written notice. I don't want to use that. It's there because there's no lien law here, so this paper is my only security.
>
> **How changes work.** If you want something different or extra, I write it down with the price and the days it adds. You sign it, or approve it by WhatsApp for small ones. Then we do it. No surprises on the final bill.
>
> **Time.** We start [date] and expect about [ ] working days. Weather, storms, shipping and late decisions move the date. If that happens, I tell you in writing.
>
> **Warranty.** Twelve months on our workmanship from the day we finish. If something we did fails, call me and I fix it. Manufacturers' warranties pass to you. It doesn't cover storm damage or wear and tear; that's what your home insurance is for.
>
> Any questions? Take it home tonight if you like. When you're ready, we both sign two copies. You keep one, I keep one."

**When they push back**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I don't do contracts. We shake hands." | "The handshake is the deal. This paper just makes sure we both remember it the same way in three months. It protects you as much as me." |
| "Why so much up front?" | "That's your materials and getting the crew booked. You'll see the supplier receipts. After that you pay as each stage is done. If you'd rather pay the supplier direct for materials, the deposit drops to [ ]%." |
| "My last contractor ran off with my deposit." | "Then pay the supplier direct for the materials, and I take a small deposit for mobilisation. You get a receipt for every dollar and photos of the work every day." |
| "Interest and stopping work? You don't trust me?" | "It's the same for every client. If you pay on time it never comes up. There's no lien law here, so this is the only protection I have." |
| "Just give me a fixed price for the whole repair." | "I'll fix the price for what I can see. For what's behind the wall, you pay actual cost up to a cap you set, and I stop and call you before I go over it." |
| "Why a change order for every little thing?" | "So there are no surprises on your final bill. Small ones take one WhatsApp: I send the price, you reply 'approved'." |
| "The warranty should cover storms." | "Storm damage is what your home insurance covers. My warranty covers my workmanship. If something I did fails, I fix it." |
| "I want a penalty if you're late." | "Fair, if it's capped and it excludes what I can't control: weather, storms, shipping, your selections. B$[ ] a week, capped at 5% of the price." |

## 5. Signing rules

**Who signs**
- **Individual owner:** the person on the title to the property. If two people own it (for example husband and wife), both sign, or the one who signs confirms in writing that they sign for both. If the client is not the owner (a tenant, a relative), get the owner's written consent before you start.
- **Company client:** use the exact registered company name and number. A director, or someone with written authority from the company, signs. Their name and title are printed under the signature, written as "for and on behalf of [Company] Ltd". For a large company or an association, ask for the authority in writing (a letter or board resolution).
- **Your own company:** once you incorporate, you sign as director "for and on behalf of [your company] Ltd". Never sign in your personal name for company work. That puts the liability back on you personally.
- **Owner abroad:** a scanned or photographed signed copy by email is the working standard. Check that the name matches their passport or driver's licence. Your attorney confirms electronic signing (section 6).
- Ask your attorney whether your company needs a company seal on any document.

**Witness:** an adult who is not a party to the contract watches the signing, then prints their name and signs. Use one for the small works agreement, the maintenance agreement and every release (Forms 2 and 3). Optional on the short form.

**Every page:** both parties initial each page of anything longer than two pages. Every blank is filled or marked N/A, so nobody can fill it in later. Any handwritten change is initialled by both.

**Copies:** two originals, both signed. Each party keeps one. If you only have one original, scan it and send the client a PDF the same day.

**WhatsApp photos:** a clear photo of every signed page, sent by WhatsApp, is acceptable as the first copy and as evidence of the deal. Then get the signed original and file it. Keep the WhatsApp chat and export it at the end of the job.

**Order of events:** signed contract, then deposit cleared, then materials ordered, then work starts. Never the other way round.

**Filing:** one folder per job, paper and cloud. Name files `[YYYY-MM-DD]_[Client]_[Document]_Rev[ ]`. Keep everything at least until the warranty ends and the job is in your licensing file. Ask your accountant how long financial records must be kept.

## 6. What a Bahamian attorney must check

**Once, before first use.** Send the whole folder at once and ask for a fixed-fee quote. Ask for tracked changes and a short note on each point below.

1. **Disputes route:** arbitration in Nassau under the Arbitration Act or the courts; which court suits small debts; whether the mediation step helps or only slows you down.
2. **Late payment interest** at [1.5]% per month, and the costs of recovery clause: enforceable as written?
3. **Right to suspend** and the termination payments (Small Works 15.2, the 10% of unfinished balance): enforceable, or at risk of being struck out as a penalty?
4. **Title to materials:** wording that lets you collect unbuilt materials from site without trespass or a fight.
5. **Liability caps and exclusions** (Short-Form 12, T&M 13, Maintenance 12, and the new Small Works 17.6): valid against homeowners? Are the carve-outs right? Does any consumer protection rule limit them? In Maintenance 12.1, do Work Order charges count as "fees paid" for the cap?
6. **WhatsApp and email** as valid notice, valid change approval and valid cap approval; scanned and electronic signatures for owners abroad.
7. **Deemed acceptance and auto-renewal:** timesheets accepted if not queried (T&M 5.4); automatic renewal and fee changes (Maintenance 1.3, 1.4).
8. **Pay-when-paid** in the subcontract (clause 5.2): settle the final wording.
9. **Releases** (Forms 2 and 3): binding as written? Do they need a witness, or to be signed as a deed?
10. **Overseas clients:** the Bahamian law and courts clause (Maintenance 16.4); payment in US dollars at par (Maintenance 3.3). Also ask your bank how incoming foreign wires are handled.
11. **Tenant and guest entry** (Maintenance 7.4): fits normal lease practice?
12. **Stamp duty:** does any of these documents need stamping?
13. **Company signing:** how your company signs, and whether a seal is needed.
14. **Housekeeping:** confirm the Small Works liability cap (17.6) and the subcontract document clause (4.2), both added on 2026-11-04.

After the review, update every file to Rev 2 with the date and record "Attorney reviewed [date], [firm]" in the log below.

**How often after that:** you review the set yourself once a year (section 7). Send it back to the attorney every 2 to 3 years, or sooner on any trigger in section 7.

## 7. How long this wording lasts

**What changes every job:** names, dates, prices, scope, payment stages, rates and the special conditions. That is what the blanks are for. Filling blanks never needs an attorney.

**What stays:** the core wording. Once an attorney has reviewed it, it should carry a small contractor for years. Don't fiddle with it job by job. Every change you make on the fly is a change nobody checked.

**Review it yourself every year,** on a fixed date (for example each January, before hurricane season planning). Ask: did any clause cause an argument? Did we miss anything on a job? Has anything below happened?

**Update immediately when:**

| Trigger | Why it matters | Who checks |
|---|---|---|
| The Construction Contractors Board is appointed and licensing starts | Your licence number and category may need to appear on every contract; there may be required terms | Attorney, BCA |
| The VAT rate or VAT rules change (rate now 10%) | Price and VAT lines, invoices, receipts | Accountant, Department of Inland Revenue |
| A lien law is passed | New rights and notice steps; the payment clauses may change | Attorney |
| Arbitration or procurement rules change | Disputes clause; government work terms | Attorney |
| A dispute or near-dispute shows a gap | That gap will cost you again on the next job | You, then attorney |
| You move to bigger or different work (over B$250,000, government, commercial, Family Island resorts, design-build) | Different risks; standard forms; bonds and insurance | Attorney |
| You incorporate | Party details, company number, signing "for and on behalf of" | Attorney |
| You register for VAT (mandatory once taxable supplies pass B$100,000 in any 12 months) | VAT TIN and VAT lines on every document | Accountant |
| You start hiring employees | Wording about "our workers", NIB, insurance | Attorney, accountant |

**Attorney re-review:** every 2 to 3 years, or on any major change above.

**Contracts already signed stay on the version they were signed on.** A new revision applies only to new contracts.
