# SOP-12 Safety, Incidents and Hurricanes
Rev 1 · 2026-11-04 · Process owner: Superintendent (the owner until hired)

**Purpose:** Everyone goes home in one piece, and every site comes through hurricane season secured.

**When:** Every working day (safety); the hour anyone is hurt or nearly hurt (incidents); June 1 to November 30 and whenever a storm is forecast (hurricanes).

**Who:** Now: the owner. Later: the superintendent runs site safety and storm shutdown; the PM handles the insurer, the client and contract notices; each sub runs its own crew under our rules.

**Forms and tools:**
- [05-field-playbook.md](../knowledge/05-field-playbook.md): Safety and Hurricane plan sections. Follow them; they are not repeated here.
- [checklists.md](../templates/checklists.md) section 5 Hurricane: 72 hours out; [daily-report.md](../templates/daily-report.md), safety section
- [subcontract-agreement.md](../templates/subcontract-agreement.md) clause 9; [02-bahamas-rules.md](../knowledge/02-bahamas-rules.md) section 5 (NIB industrial benefits); [06-money.md](../knowledge/06-money.md) (insurance, reserves)

**Steps:**
Safety, every site:
1. Set up before work starts: first aid kit, fire extinguisher, drinking water, site rules posted, emergency list (919 or 911, nearest hospital or clinic, client, insurer, your phone).
2. Walk the site before anyone works: falls, scaffolds, ladders, trenches over 5 feet, cords and temporary power, heat. Fix it or stop that work.
3. Give a 5-minute toolbox talk every day. Log the topic and who attended.
4. Enforce PPE, and fall protection above 6 feet. Anyone unsafe, drunk or high leaves the site. No exceptions, friends included.
5. Only licensed electricians touch circuits, and circuits are locked out first.

Incidents:
6. Someone hurt: first aid, call 919 or 911, secure the scene, photograph it. Notify the client and your insurer the same day.
7. Your own employee: file NIB form B44 (Employer's Report on Accident); NIB claims are made within 3 months. A sub's employee: the sub reports to NIB, and to you the same day (subcontract clause 9.5).
8. Write an incident report the same day: who, what, when, where, witnesses, photos. Near misses get one too.
9. Within 48 hours: find the cause, fix it, and give a toolbox talk on it.
10. Theft or damage to a neighbour's property: photos, a police report for theft, and the insurer notified within the policy's notice period.

Hurricanes:
11. By June 1 each year: confirm named-storm cover on your policies, update the crew and sub contact list, schedule roof dry-in and openings before August where you can, and top up the hurricane cash reserve.
12. Every job with work between June 1 and November 30 has a written hurricane plan in its folder from day one (SOP-06): what gets secured, where materials go, who does each task.
13. In season, check the Bahamas Department of Meteorology and the US National Hurricane Center every morning.
14. When a storm is forecast, run the 5-day, 72-hour and 48-hour timeline in 05 and the 72-hour checklist. Photograph the whole site before you leave. Release the crew in time to protect their families.
15. After the storm: safety walk before anyone works. Photograph damage. Notify the insurer and the client in writing within the notice periods. Send the extension of time notice within 7 days (small works clause 5.3).
16. Price post-storm work at your normal margin. Pass on real material increases with the supplier's invoice. No gouging.

**Done when:** Every working day has a logged safety walk and toolbox talk; every incident has a same-day report and a fix; every in-season job has a hurricane plan on file.

**Records:** 07 Daily Reports: toolbox talk log, incident reports, NIB B44 copies. 03 Permits and Drawings: job hurricane plan. 08 Photos: incident, pre-storm and post-storm photos. Company/Insurance: claims and correspondence.

**Mistakes this prevents:**
- A fall or electrocution on a site that had no rules.
- An injury claim landing on you because a sub's worker had no NIB cover.
- Materials and scaffolds becoming missiles in a storm, and an insurer refusing the claim.
- A reputation lost to post-storm gouging.

**Review triggers:** Any injury or near miss; any storm that hits a site; insurer changes to named-storm cover; new NIB or Health and Safety at Work rules; before every June 1.
