# SOP-08 Quality Control and Inspections
Rev 1 · 2026-11-04 · Process owner: Superintendent (the owner until hired)

**Purpose:** Nothing gets covered, poured or paid for until it has been checked against the drawings and passed.

**When:** Before every hold point, before every concrete pour, before any work is closed in, and when a sub finishes an area.

**Who:** Now: the owner. Later: the superintendent inspects; the PM books Building Control and the engineer.

**Forms and tools:**
- [05-field-playbook.md](../knowledge/05-field-playbook.md): quality hold points table, failures seen again and again
- [checklists.md](../templates/checklists.md) section 4 Before any concrete pour
- [daily-report.md](../templates/daily-report.md), inspections section; [job-cost-tracker.xlsx](../tools/job-cost-tracker.xlsx), Look-Ahead
- [02-bahamas-rules.md](../knowledge/02-bahamas-rules.md) sections 7 and 8, permits and the Building Code

**Steps:**
1. At job start, list the job's hold points: the permit's inspection stages plus the hold point table in 05. Put each one on the Look-Ahead.
2. Book Building Control inspections as soon as the date is firm. Ask how much notice they need and allow for it.
3. Before each hold point, inspect it yourself against the stamped drawings, not against the sub's word.
4. Photograph it with a tape measure in the shot: bar spacing, laps, cover, straps, depths.
5. Fix every defect before you call the inspector. The inspector is not your checker.
6. Record the inspection in the daily report: time, inspector's name, result, any items noted.
7. No pour and no cover until both checks pass. Tell the crew and the concrete driver the rule.
8. Every pour: delivery ticket strength matches the drawing; no water added on site without your OK; vibrate; keep it wet and cure 7 days; cylinders where the engineer requires them.
9. Wet areas: flood test the membrane for 24 hours before any tile goes down.
10. MEP: licensed electricians and plumbers only. Inspected before covering. Photograph everything inside walls before they are closed.
11. Approve the first piece of each finish (first tile wall, first coat of paint, first door hung) before the sub does the rest.
12. Reject defective work in writing the same day. The sub has 48 hours to fix it; then you fix it and backcharge (subcontract clause 11).
13. If an inspection fails: record the items, fix them, book the re-inspection. The cost goes to whoever caused it.
14. Send every structural question to the engineer in writing. Never guess a bar size, beam, footing or connection.

Rule: never pay or offer "speed money" for an inspection. If an inspection is late, follow up in writing with Building Control and record it as a delay under the contract.

**Done when:** Every hold point on the job has your dated check, a passed inspection where one is required, and photos on file.

**Records:** 03 Permits and Drawings: inspection results and re-inspections. 07 Daily Reports: inspection entries, rejection notices to subs. 08 Photos: hold point photos.

**Mistakes this prevents:**
- Straps forgotten before the tie beam pour, then retrofitted badly.
- Hollow cores, low cover and watered-down concrete hidden forever.
- Paying a sub for work you later tear out.
- Failed inspections that cost days and your name with Building Control.

**Review triggers:** Any failed inspection; any callback for a hidden defect; a new edition or amendment of the Bahamas Building Code; a change in how Building Control books or runs inspections.
