# SOP-02 Site Visit and Estimating
Rev 1 · 2026-11-04 · Process owner: Estimator (the owner until hired)

**Purpose:** Predict what the job will really cost, with nothing missing, before any price goes to anyone.

**When:** A site visit is booked under SOP-01.

**Who:** Now: the owner. Later: the estimator prices it; the PM who will run the job reviews it before it goes out.

**Forms and tools:**
- [estimator.xlsx](../tools/estimator.xlsx): Assumptions, Landed Cost, Job Estimate, Markup Margin, Cash Flow, Labour Cost
- [cost-code-book.xlsx](../tools/cost-code-book.xlsx): Cost Codes, Unit Cost Book; [07-cost-codes.md](../knowledge/07-cost-codes.md)
- [job-cost-tracker.xlsx](../tools/job-cost-tracker.xlsx), sheet Sub Bids (start the job's copy now)
- [04-estimating-and-materials.md](../knowledge/04-estimating-and-materials.md); [checklists.md](../templates/checklists.md) section 3 Before you send a quote

**Steps:**
1. Prepare the day before: read the client's notes and any drawings, write your questions, charge the phone, pack tape, laser measure, level, torch and notepad.
2. Arrive on time. Walk the whole area with the client. Let them talk first.
3. Measure everything you will price. Write dimensions on a sketch on site, not from memory later.
4. Photograph every wall, floor and ceiling, all existing damage, the meters and panel, the access route, parking and the neighbours' side. Existing-damage photos protect you later.
5. Note truck and skip bin access, storage, power, water, slope, and what you cannot see: inside walls, under floors, the roof.
6. Send the client your open questions in writing within 24 hours.
7. Pick the contract type: lump sum if you can see the whole scope; time and materials with a cap if you cannot see the problem until you open it up ([03-contracts.md](../knowledge/03-contracts.md)).
8. Break the job into work items in build sequence ([05-field-playbook.md](../knowledge/05-field-playbook.md)). Give each item a cost code and cost type, for example 09-30-S for tile by a sub.
9. Take off quantities in Job Estimate. Show the math on every line.
10. Price materials landed and delivered (Landed Cost sheet). Get written, dated supplier quotes and note how long they are valid.
11. Send each trade's scope to 3 subs (SOP-04). Log every written quote in Sub Bids.
12. Add general conditions (list in 04). Price Family Island logistics as their own lines, never as a percentage guess.
13. Add contingency: 3% to 5% for clear scope, 10% to 15% for renovation with unknowns.
14. Set the price on margin, not markup (Markup Margin sheet: Price = Cost / (1 - margin)). Apply the minimum job charge. Add VAT only if registered.
15. Run the Cash Flow sheet against your proposed payment schedule. Cash must stay positive at every milestone. If it goes negative, change the schedule, not your bank balance.
16. Compare cost per sq ft or per room with the Unit Cost Book. If it is far off, find out why before it goes out.
17. Run checklist section 3. On jobs over B$25,000, leave it 24 hours and re-check with fresh eyes.
18. Finish the estimate within 5 working days of the visit (10 for jobs over B$50,000), or tell the client the date it will be ready.

**Done when:** The estimate is saved in the job folder, every checklist item is ticked, every number has a dated quote or a cost-book source, and the cash flow stays positive.

**Records:** 02 Estimate: site sketch, estimate file, supplier quotes, sub quotes, question list. Site photos to 08 Photos. Status updated in Leads & Quotes.

**Mistakes this prevents:**
- Missing items. Most bad estimates fail on what was left out, not on unit prices.
- Pricing shelf cost instead of landed cost.
- Confusing markup with margin and underpricing every job.
- A payment schedule that makes you finance the client.

**Review triggers:** Any job finishing more than 3 points below its estimated margin; big supplier or freight price swings; every 10 completed jobs (update the Unit Cost Book and this SOP).
