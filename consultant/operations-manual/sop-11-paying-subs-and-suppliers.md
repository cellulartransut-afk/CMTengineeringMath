# SOP-11 Paying Subs and Suppliers
Rev 1 · 2026-11-04 · Process owner: Office Admin (the owner until hired; the owner approves)

**Purpose:** Pay subs and suppliers correctly and on time, from client money, with proof, so the best people keep working for you.

**When:** Weekly pay run every Friday. Invoices received by Wednesday 5 pm go in that week's run.

**Who:** Now: the owner. Later: the superintendent confirms the work is accepted; the PM approves the amount; office admin checks documents, pays and files; the owner releases payments.

**Forms and tools:**
- [subcontract-agreement.md](../templates/subcontract-agreement.md), clause 5 (payment and retention) and clause 11 (backcharges)
- [receipts-and-releases.md](../templates/receipts-and-releases.md)
- [office-tracker.xlsx](../tools/office-tracker.xlsx), Subs & Documents; [job-cost-tracker.xlsx](../tools/job-cost-tracker.xlsx): Commitments, Actual Costs, Billing

**Steps:**
1. Get the sub's invoice for the stages in their subcontract. No invoice, no payment.
2. Walk the work with the invoice in hand. Pay only for work done and accepted. List defects in writing; defective work is not paid until fixed (clause 5.4).
3. Check the Billing sheet: has the client paid us for that work? If yes, pay within 7 days of the client's payment (clause 5.2). If the client is holding payment because of our fault, or for reasons unrelated to this sub's work, pay within 30 days of the sub's invoice anyway.
4. Check the sub's documents in Subs & Documents before every payment: business licence, NIB letter of good standing, insurance certificate, crew list. If any has expired, ask for the renewal and hold the payment until it arrives. Have your attorney confirm the subcontract wording supports the hold.
5. Calculate the amount in the job's tracker (example below). Deduct a backcharge only after the written notice and 48 hours under clause 11.
6. Approve: for now the owner approves every payment.
7. Pay by bank transfer or business cheque, to the sub's business name. Never cash. Never pay a sub's workers directly.
8. Get a signed receipt and release (receipts-and-releases.md) for every payment, the same day. Final release with the final payment.
9. Code the payment in Actual Costs to job number and cost code, for example 09-30-S.
10. Retention: release half at practical completion of their work and the owner's acceptance; release the balance at the end of the defects period if their defects are fixed (clause 5.3). Put both dates in the calendar.
11. Suppliers: match each invoice to its purchase order and signed delivery note. Query errors within 2 working days. Pay by the due date to keep your 30-day credit.
12. If VAT registered: keep a valid VAT invoice from every VAT-registered sub and supplier for your input claims.
13. If a sub's workers say they have not been paid, ask the sub for proof of wages paid before the next run. Record it.

| Example: tile sub, B$5,000 lump sum | B$ |
|---|---|
| Work accepted to date (60%) | 3,000 |
| Less 10% retention (3,000 x 10%) | (300) |
| Less paid before (30% stage: 1,500 less 150 retention) | (1,350) |
| Less backcharge for cleanup, after written notice | (100) |
| **Pay this run** | **1,250** |

**Done when:** Every approved invoice is paid on the pay run, coded, and backed by a signed receipt and release, with retention dates in the calendar.

**Records:** 06 Invoices and Payments: sub and supplier invoices, payment confirmations, signed receipts and releases. 04 Subs and POs: copies of the sub documents checked at payment.

**Mistakes this prevents:**
- Paying a sub ahead of the work or ahead of the client's money.
- Paying a sub whose insurance or NIB has lapsed, then inheriting their claim.
- A sub, or the sub's workers, claiming they were never paid.
- Paying a supplier invoice twice, or for goods that never came.

**Review triggers:** Any payment dispute with a sub or supplier; attorney changes to subcontract clause 5; you hire someone else to make payments.
