# SOP-09 Change Orders
Rev 1 · 2026-11-04 · Process owner: Project Manager (the owner until hired)

**Purpose:** No extra work happens until it is priced, timed and signed.

**When:** The client asks for anything not in the contract scope; you find a hidden condition; the inspector or engineer requires a change; a client selection costs more than its allowance.

**Who:** Now: the owner. Later: the superintendent spots and reports it; the PM prices it, issues it and gets the signature.

**Forms and tools:**
- [change-order.md](../templates/change-order.md)
- [job-cost-tracker.xlsx](../tools/job-cost-tracker.xlsx): Change Orders, Budget & Cost Report, Billing
- [estimator.xlsx](../tools/estimator.xlsx) for pricing; [03-contracts.md](../knowledge/03-contracts.md), change orders
- [owner-contract-small-works.md](../templates/owner-contract-small-works.md): clauses 3.3 (allowances), 5.3 (extensions of time), 7 (changes), 8 (unforeseen conditions). Check the matching clauses in the short-form or T&M agreement if you used one.

**Steps:**
1. Stop. Don't do the extra work, and tell the sub not to. Subs take instructions only from you.
2. Confirm the request or the finding in writing the same day: "As discussed today, you asked us to ... We will send a price by [date]."
3. Hidden condition: stop the affected work, photograph it, notify the client in writing the same day, and propose a change (clause 8).
4. Emergency (safety risk, water coming in): do only the minimum to make it safe. Notify the client the same day. Issue the change order within 48 hours (clause 7.2).
5. Price it within 48 hours for small changes, 5 working days for large ones. Use the same unit costs and margin as the base estimate, plus a fair fee for disruption. Get the sub's price in writing first.
6. Price the time too. If it adds days, say how many and give the new completion date.
7. Write the change order: number it in order (CO-01, CO-02), describe it exactly, attach a sketch or photos, state price, VAT, time and how it is paid.
8. If the change needs materials, collect at least the materials cost on signing. Otherwise bill it with the next stage payment.
9. Get the client's signature before the work starts: on paper, or a signed copy sent back by WhatsApp or email. A verbal yes is not a change order.
10. Issue a written variation to the sub before they start the extra (subcontract clause 8).
11. Log it in Change Orders the day it is signed. Update the budget, the schedule and the look-ahead.
12. Bill it on the next invoice (SOP-10).
13. If the client says no, don't do it. Record the refusal in the daily report.
14. Go over the change order log with the client every week. Nothing waits until the end.

**Done when:** Every change has a change order signed before the work, a matching sub variation, and appears in the log, the budget and an invoice.

**Records:** 05 Change Orders: signed change orders, pricing backup, sub variations, photos of hidden conditions. Same-day confirmations in 01 Contract with the job's WhatsApp export (SOP-15).

**Mistakes this prevents:**
- Doing extra work free because "we'll sort it out at the end". At the end you have no leverage.
- Arguments about price after the work is done.
- A client holding you to the old finish date after adding work.

**Review triggers:** Any change order unpaid or disputed; any extra work done without a signature; repeated client complaints about how changes are priced.
