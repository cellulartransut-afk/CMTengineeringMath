# 05. Field playbook: project manager and superintendent

As of 2026-11-03. Structural sizes, rebar, connections and concrete strengths come from the stamped drawings. This file tells you what to check, not what to design.

## Two jobs, one person (for now)

| Project manager | Superintendent |
|---|---|
| Contract, scope, changes | Site, people, daily production |
| Money: budget, billing, collections, sub payments | Quality: checks every hold point |
| Schedule: master schedule, procurement, lead times | Safety: runs the site safely every day |
| Client: meetings, decisions, updates | Subs: coordination, sequence, cleanup |
| Documents: contracts, permits, submittals | Records: daily report, photos, deliveries |

At the start you are both. The super's order of priorities every day: **safety, quality, schedule, cost.** Never trade the first for the last.

## Job life cycle

1. **Lead.** Who is the client, can they pay, what do they want, by when? Check them (see `templates/checklists.md`).
2. **Site visit.** Measure, photograph, note access, power, water, neighbours, existing damage.
3. **Estimate and proposal.** `04-estimating-and-materials.md`, `templates/quote-proposal.md`.
4. **Contract.** Signed before work. Deposit received before buying.
5. **Preconstruction.** Permit, drawings, subs signed, materials ordered, schedule, site plan, kickoff.
6. **Build.** Daily system below.
7. **Closeout.** Punch list, inspections, certificate of occupancy, final payment, documents.
8. **Warranty.** Answer callbacks fast. A quick fix makes a client for life.

## Preconstruction checklist

- [ ] Contract signed, deposit cleared
- [ ] Permit issued and posted on site (or confirmed not required, in writing)
- [ ] Current drawings, stamped where required; you have read every sheet
- [ ] Survey or boundary pegs located; setbacks confirmed (get a surveyor for new work)
- [ ] Existing services located: water, power, septic, cable
- [ ] Site plan: deliveries, storage, skip bin, toilet, parking, neighbours, security
- [ ] Subcontracts signed, documents collected (licence, NIB, insurance, crew list)
- [ ] Long-lead items ordered; lead times on the schedule
- [ ] Schedule built and sent to client and subs
- [ ] Budget set from the estimate; cost codes ready
- [ ] Kickoff meeting with client: who decides, how changes work, selection deadlines, payment dates, communication (weekly update with photos)
- [ ] Hurricane plan if any work falls June 1 to November 30

## Typical sequence: Bahamian concrete block house

Use it as your schedule template and your estimating checklist.

1. Permit, set out (pegs, string lines, levels)
2. Excavate footings
3. **Hold: footing inspection** (yours, then Building Control)
4. Pour footings
5. Block up to slab level (stem walls); fill cores at rebar
6. Backfill and compact in layers; termite treatment
7. Under-slab plumbing and electrical sleeves; vapour barrier; slab rebar or mesh on chairs
8. **Hold: slab inspection**
9. Pour slab; cure
10. Block walls to tie-beam height; vertical bars and lintels at openings; fill cores
11. Tie beam (belt course) formwork and rebar; **hurricane straps set in place before the pour**
12. **Hold: tie beam inspection**
13. Pour tie beam; cure
14. Roof framing (trusses or rafters), every strap fixed, bracing
15. **Hold: roof framing and strapping inspection**
16. Sheathing, underlayment, roofing: dry-in
17. Windows and exterior doors with flashing and anchoring
18. Electrical, plumbing and AC rough-in by licensed trades
19. **Hold: rough-in inspections**
20. Stucco and plaster inside and out; ceilings
21. Waterproofing wet areas; tile
22. Cabinets, doors, trim
23. Paint
24. Fixtures, devices, AC equipment, appliances
25. **Final inspections**; certificate of occupancy; BPL and Water and Sewerage connections
26. Punch list, final clean, handover

## Quality hold points: what to check before you let anyone continue

| Hold point | Check |
|---|---|
| Footings | Depth and width per drawing; bottom firm, clean, no loose fill or water; rebar size, spacing, laps, 3 inch cover on chairs or blocks, not on the dirt; dowels for walls in the right place |
| Block walls | Plumb, level, line; mortar joints full; vertical bars at the right spacing and lapped; cleanouts at the bottom of grouted cores; lintels and columns per drawing |
| Core fill | Cores filled solid, no voids; rodded or vibrated; filled in lifts, not dumped from the top of a tall wall |
| Tie beam | Bar size and count, stirrups, laps at corners, cover; **straps or anchors set at every truss position before the pour**; formwork braced; top level |
| Concrete (every pour) | Ordered strength matches the drawing; check delivery ticket; **no water added on site** beyond what the supplier allows; vibrate; finish; keep wet and cure 7 days; cylinders taken where the engineer requires |
| Roof framing | Truss spacing, bracing, every strap nailed with the right nails and count; sheathing nailing pattern at edges and field per drawing |
| Roofing | Underlayment laps; drip edge; high-wind nailing or fastening pattern from the manufacturer |
| Windows and doors | Product approved for the opening; anchors type and spacing per product approval; flashing and sealant; operate and lock |
| Wet areas | Waterproof membrane, flood tested 24 hours before tile; falls to drain |
| MEP | Licensed trades only; inspected before covering; photos of everything inside walls before closing |

**Failures seen again and again in The Bahamas, avoid them:**
- Straps forgotten before the tie beam pour, then retrofitted badly
- Hollow or half-filled cores
- Water added to concrete on site to make it easy to place (weakens it)
- Weak roof-to-wall connections (the main cause of roof loss in hurricanes)
- No flashing at windows, leaks into block walls
- Rusted rebar and spalling from low cover or beach sand
- Septic too close to a well
- Termite damage in untreated wood touching soil or block

## The super's daily routine

| Time | Do |
|---|---|
| Before start | Arrive first. Walk the site. Check safety: edges, scaffolds, ladders, power cords, trenches. |
| Start | Toolbox talk (5 minutes, one topic). Confirm who is on site against the plan. |
| Morning | Check deliveries against delivery notes. Inspect yesterday's work. Answer sub questions. |
| Midday | Photos of progress and anything to be covered up. Check against schedule. Water and shade in the heat. |
| Afternoon | Plan tomorrow. Update the three-week look-ahead. Call subs for tomorrow. |
| End | Site secured, materials covered, tools locked. **Daily report written** (`templates/daily-report.md`). |

## Weekly routine

- Client update: progress, photos, decisions needed with dates, money status.
- Three-week look-ahead updated and sent to subs.
- Cost check: budget vs committed vs spent vs forecast to finish, per cost code.
- Invoices out on schedule. Collections followed up the day they are late.
- Sub payments for accepted work, after the client pays for it.
- Change order log updated and signed.

## Running subs

- Pre-start meeting: scope, drawings, schedule, crew size, site rules, quality standard, how they get paid.
- Hold them to the schedule they agreed. Daily check: are they here, with enough people, doing the right thing?
- Inspect their work before they leave an area. Reject defects in writing the same day.
- Backcharges: written notice, 48 hours to fix, then you fix and deduct. Keep photos.
- Pay accepted work promptly when you are paid. The best subs work for the contractors who pay.
- Never let them deal with your client directly on price or extras.

## Running the client

- One point of contact on each side.
- Every decision in writing, even if just a WhatsApp confirmation.
- Selections (tile, fixtures, paint, cabinets) with deadlines tied to the schedule. Late selection means time extension, in writing.
- Bad news early, with a fix. Clients forgive problems; they don't forgive surprises.

## Safety

The most common ways people get hurt or killed on Bahamian sites: falls from roofs, scaffolds and ladders; things falling on people; trench collapse; electric shock; heat.

- **Falls:** guardrails or harnesses on roofs and edges; scaffolds on firm base, braced, planked, with guardrails; ladders tied off, 1 out for every 4 up, 3 feet above the landing.
- **Trenches:** over 5 feet deep, shore or slope the sides; nobody in an unsupported trench.
- **Electrical:** temporary power with ground-fault protection; no damaged cords; lock out before working on circuits; licensed electrician only.
- **Heat:** water on site always, shade, rest breaks, start early in summer.
- **PPE:** hard hats, boots, gloves, eye protection, hearing protection for cutting.
- **First aid kit** and an emergency contact list on every site. Emergency numbers 919 or 911.
- **If someone is hurt:** first aid, call emergency services, secure the scene, photograph, notify the client and insurer. For your employees, file NIB form B44. Write it up the same day.
- Remove anyone who works unsafely or is drunk or high. No exceptions.

## Hurricane plan (every job June 1 to November 30)

**Before the season:** schedule roof dry-in and openings before August if you can. Confirm insurance covers named storms. Keep the crew contact list current.

**When a storm is forecast (watch the Bahamas Department of Meteorology and the US National Hurricane Center):**

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| 5 days out | Stop non-essential deliveries. Check what must be secured. Talk to the client. |
| 72 hours | Secure or remove loose materials, debris, skip bins. Lower or tie down scaffolding. Remove signs and banners. Fasten roof sheathing and cover open roofs. Install shutters. Fuel the generator and vehicles. Photograph the whole site (for insurance). Back up documents. |
| 48 hours | Site closed. Tools and equipment removed or locked. Crew released to protect their own families. |
| After | Safety walk before anyone works: power lines, structural damage, standing water. Photograph damage. Notify insurer and client in writing within the notice period. Send a written extension-of-time notice under the contract. Watch for price rises on materials and update the client. |

**Don't** gouge prices after a storm. Your reputation lasts longer than the storm.
