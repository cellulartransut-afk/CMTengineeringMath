# 01. Roadmap: paper contractor at 18 to owner of assets

As of 2026-11-03.

## The business in one line

You sell management: you find work, price it, contract it, buy it out to subs, control it, and collect. Your profit is the gap between what the client pays and what the job costs, minus your overhead. Everything in this file is about making that gap big, safe and repeatable, then using it to buy assets that pay you without you on site.

## First principles

1. **What a paper contractor actually is.** You hold the contract with the client. Subs do the physical work. You carry the risk for all of it: price, quality, schedule, safety, and paying everyone. Subcontracting the work does not subcontract the liability.
2. **Your product is trust.** The Bahamian market has many contractors who don't answer the phone, don't show up, don't write anything down and don't finish. The Bahamian Contractors Association itself calls the market a "free for all" until licensing starts. Being the one who is reliable is your edge. It costs nothing but discipline.
3. **Your weakness is field knowledge.** At 18 with no site years, subs and clients can take advantage of you. You fix this by spending real time on sites, walking every job, and learning what good work looks like. Your CM degree teaches the system. The site teaches the reality. You need both.
4. **Cash kills more contractors than bad work.** Price right, get deposits, bill ahead of spend, pay subs from client money. You never finance the client's job.
5. **Assets are where the "money works for me" part comes from.** Contracting pays for your time and your team's time. Rentals, equipment, service contracts and development pay without your hours. Contracting is the machine that funds the assets.

## The money math for your first year

Typical small job, bathroom renovation in Nassau (illustration; your prices come from your own quotes):

| Line | B$ |
|---|---|
| Sub labour (tile, plumber, carpenter, painter) | 4,500 |
| Materials, landed and delivered | 4,000 |
| Skip bin, trucking, cleanup | 400 |
| Contingency 5% | 445 |
| **Direct cost** | **9,345** |
| Price at 22% gross margin = cost / (1 - 0.22) | **11,981** |
| Gross profit | 2,636 |

If your overhead (phone, fuel, vehicle, insurance, software, accounting, marketing) is B$2,000 a month, that is B$24,000 a year. To pay overhead plus pay yourself B$36,000:

- Gross profit needed = 24,000 + 36,000 = B$60,000
- Revenue needed at 22% margin = 60,000 / 0.22 = **B$272,700**
- That is about 23 jobs this size, or about 2 a month.

That is a real, reachable first-year target for a disciplined small contractor. Run your own numbers in `consultant/tools/estimator.xlsx`.

## The stages

Do not move to the next stage until you pass its gate. Gates are what keep you from growing into a bigger failure.

### Stage 0. Foundation (months 0 to 3)

Goal: be legal, set up, learning, with a sub bench and your first small jobs.

| Week | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pick a name. Check it is free at the Registrar General. Decide sole trader or company (see `02-bahamas-rules.md`; company recommended before any job over about B$25,000). |
| 1 to 2 | Register the business. Apply for the business licence with Inland Revenue. Open a business-only bank account. |
| 2 | Business phone with WhatsApp Business, business email, simple logo, Google Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram page. |
| 2 to 6 | Build the sub bench: two subs per trade. Visit their current jobs before you hire them. Collect business licence, NIB good standing, insurance. Log them in `my-business/subs.md`. |
| 2 to 12 | Get on a site: helper, assistant to a super, weekends or holidays. Watch block, rebar, concrete, roofing, finishes. Ask why. |
| 4 to 12 | First jobs, B$500 to B$10,000: painting, repairs, doors and windows, fencing, small bathrooms, make-ready for rentals. Use the contract templates every time, even for family. |
| 8 to 12 | Join the Bahamian Contractors Association. Get public liability insurance quotes. Set up job costing. |

**Gate to Stage 1:** registered and licensed, business bank account, 5 jobs completed and paid, written contract on every job, 6+ trades on the bench with documents.

### Stage 1. Paper contractor, small renovation and maintenance (year 1 to 2)

Target: B$150,000 to B$400,000 revenue, 20% to 30% gross margin.

Best markets for you now:
- Landlords and property managers: make-ready between tenants, repairs, painting. Repeat work.
- Short-term rental owners, especially owners living abroad: they need a trustworthy local who answers and documents with photos. Repeat work.
- Real estate agents: repairs before sale and after the buyer's inspection.
- Homeowners: bathrooms, kitchens, roofs, hurricane shutters, generator pads, fences, decks.
- Post-hurricane repair, priced fairly. Insurance adjusters and claims work.
- Small commercial fit-outs and repairs for offices and shops.

Rules: no job bigger than 3 times your largest completed job. No job you cannot get a 30% or larger deposit on. No Family Island job until you have priced the logistics properly (see `04`).

**Gate to Stage 2:** 15+ jobs paid, average gross margin 20%+, 3 months of overhead in reserve, repeat clients, no open disputes, photos and testimonials for every job.

### Stage 2. General contractor, self-perform one trade (year 2 to 5)

Target: B$500,000 to B$1.5M revenue.

- Hire a working foreman and build one crew in a trade you understand and that has a high labour share and low equipment cost: painting, tile, drywall and ceilings, or block and concrete. Self-performing captures the sub's margin and gives you control of the schedule on the critical path.
- Take whole-house renovations, additions, small new houses with stamped drawings, and small commercial.
- Register on the government eProcurement portal. The Public Procurement Act, 2023 allows preferences for small, youth-owned and Family Island businesses. Government pays slowly; price the wait and never let one ministry cheque decide your payroll.
- Get your first bank facility and a relationship banker. Start yearly reviewed financial statements; you will need them for bonds.
- Apply for the contractor licence the day the Construction Contractors Board opens, with your project records.

**Gate to Stage 3:** two years of clean financial statements, net profit 8%+ after paying yourself, a foreman or PM who can run a job without you, bank facility in place.

### Stage 3. GC plus first assets (year 5 to 10)

Target: B$1.5M to B$5M contracting revenue plus asset income.

Start the assets that pay without your hours, funded by contracting profit, not by risking the company:
- **Build-to-rent.** Duplex to fourplex. If you live in one unit, check first-time homeowner VAT relief (the 2026/27 budget proposed extending it to 2 to 4 unit buildings with one owner-occupied unit; confirm it passed and how Inland Revenue applies it). You build at cost, which is your edge over every other buyer.
- **Service contracts.** Annual maintenance agreements with property managers, rental owners, gated communities, small commercial. Monthly fee, recurring revenue.
- **Equipment and scaffold rental.** Start with what you already use and rent it out when idle: scaffolding, mixers, compactors, generators, a skid steer. Hurricane recovery spikes demand.

### Stage 4. Holding company (year 10+)

- The contracting company runs under a general manager and PMs you trained.
- A separate company owns the real estate. Another owns the equipment and leases it to the contractor and to others. Separate companies separate the risk.
- You allocate capital: development land, build-to-sell or build-to-rent, partnerships.

## Other businesses where people and money work for you

| Business | Capital needed | Risk | Recurring income | Fit for you and when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Property maintenance company | Low | Low | High (monthly contracts) | Start in Stage 1. Natural extension. |
| Owner's representative for owners abroad building on Family Islands | Low | Medium (reputation) | Medium | Stage 2 to 3, once you have a track record. They pay you to watch their contractor. |
| Build-to-rent units | High | Medium | High | Stage 3. Your cost edge as builder. |
| Equipment and scaffold rental | Medium to high | Medium | High | Stage 3. Buy used, rent when idle. |
| Materials import and resale to Family Island contractors | Medium | Medium (inventory, freight) | Medium | Stage 3 if you are already landing containers. |
| Development (land, subdivide, build, sell) | Very high | High | Low (lumpy) | Stage 4. Biggest money and biggest risk. |
| Block plant or ready-mix | Very high | High | Medium | Only with partners and proven demand. Not early. |
