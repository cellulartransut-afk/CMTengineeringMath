# 02. Bahamas rules: licensing, company, tax, NIB, permits, procurement, labour

As of 2026-11-04. Rates and laws change. Every item says who confirms it. Sources are listed at the bottom. Where sources disagree or are secondary, it says so.

## 1. Contractor licensing: Construction Contractors Act

**Status.** The Act passed in 2016. It sets up a Construction Contractors Board to license and register contractors and tradespeople by category and scale of work, and to discipline bad work and misconduct. A registrar was appointed, but as of April 2026 reporting the Board had still not been appointed, so nobody is licensed under the Act yet. The BCA has pushed for it for years, including a threatened judicial review in 2024 and a petition in February 2026.

**Board makeup (planned).** Ten members: six nominated by the BCA, three appointed by the minister on BCA advice, plus the Director of Public Works.

**Grandfathering.** BCA reported the window for existing contractors runs about 2026 to 2028, starting when the Board is announced.

**Categories (from the 2016 Bill, may differ in the final text).** About 21, including General, Residential Building, Commercial Building, Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC, Roofing, Masonry, Roads, Landscape, Civil Marine, Specialty and Owner-Builder.

**Proposed requirements.** Proof you completed a project in the category you apply for, a valid business licence, no outstanding complaints.

**What this means for you.**
- Today you can trade as a contractor with a business licence. Licensed electricians and plumbers are still required for their trades, and their work is inspected; you hire them as subs.
- Start your licensing file now: for every job keep the contract, permit (if any), photos before, during and after, final invoice, proof of payment, client letter. When the Board opens, you apply with evidence, not stories.
- Join the BCA. Credibility, network, and early news on the Board.

**Confirm with:** Ministry of Public Works, the BCA, Official Gazette.

## 2. Company setup

| Option | What it is | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader with registered business name | You and the business are the same legal person. Simple. | Only for the first few very small jobs. |
| Company limited by shares (Companies Act) | Separate legal person. Protects personal assets from most business debts and claims (banks will still want your personal guarantee on loans). | Before any job over about B$25,000, before hiring employees, before government work. Recommended early, because construction liability is high. |

- Filed with the Registrar General's Department, usually through a Bahamian attorney or registered agent. Costs vary with the agent; packages including government fees have been quoted around B$1,000 to B$2,000. Get two quotes. There is an annual government fee to stay in good standing.
- At 18 you are an adult and can sign contracts and be a director. Your attorney confirms this when incorporating.
- Later (Stage 4) use separate companies: one for contracting, one for real estate, one for equipment.

**Confirm with:** Registrar General's Department, a Bahamian attorney.

## 3. Business licence: Business Licence Act, 2023

- In force from 1 July 2023. Replaced the 2010 Act. Apply and renew with the Department of Inland Revenue.
- Tax is on gross turnover of the previous year, not on profit. "Turnover" includes rent and royalties.
- Reported bands (law-firm summaries of the Bill; confirm against the Second Schedule of the Act):

| Prior-year turnover | Reported rate |
|---|---|
| Up to B$100,000 | Exempt from the tax (you still need the licence) |
| Above B$100,000 to B$500,000 | 0.5% |
| Above B$500,000 to B$5M | 0.75% |
| Above B$5M | 1.25% (audited statements required above B$5M) |

- Freeport: you also need a Grand Bahama Port Authority business licence to trade in the Port area.

- **Deadlines (reported):** the annual business licence return is due by **31 January**, with the tax payment deadline moved to **31 March** (Tribune Business). PwC's summary of the 2023 Act puts filing and payment on or before 31 March, and another guide says 31 March for both, so confirm the current year's dates. Reported penalties: B$100 for late filing, 10% of tax owed for late payment, plus interest on payments 30 or more days late.

**Confirm with:** Department of Inland Revenue.

## 4. VAT

- Standard rate **10%** (cut from 12% in 2022). Construction services are standard-rated.
- **Mandatory registration** once your taxable supplies pass **B$100,000 in any 12 months**. You can register voluntarily below that.
- Registered: you charge 10% on invoices, file returns, and claim back VAT you paid on materials and subs (input VAT), subject to the rules.
- Not registered: you cannot charge VAT, and the VAT you pay on materials is a cost. Price it in.
- **Filing (reported):** returns are filed online through Inland Revenue's Online Tax Administration (OTA) system. Quarterly if annual turnover is under B$5M, monthly at B$5M or more. Return and payment are due within 21 days of the end of the period. No extensions to pay. A nil return is still required for a period with no sales.
- **Should you register early?** Yes if most clients are businesses or government (they want VAT invoices and can claim it back). Maybe not if most clients are homeowners (your price looks 10% higher than unregistered competitors). Track your rolling 12-month turnover every month so you register on time.
- **2025 change for big projects.** The government moved to restrict input VAT claims on "major construction" (reported as projects of B$1M or more, and work like dredging, reclamation, docks and marinas), except where the business supplies real property as a taxable activity. The BCA argued the threshold is too low. Check the enacted text before pricing any job near B$1M.
- **First-time homeowners (your clients).** A VAT rebate on construction services and materials for first-time homeowners exists (Section 55A, VAT Act; up to B$40,000 under the 2022 measures). The 2026/27 budget proposed extending first-time homeowner relief to duplexes through fourplexes with one owner-occupied unit and raising the real property tax exemption from B$500,000 to B$600,000. Tell qualifying clients about it; it helps them say yes.

**Confirm with:** Department of Inland Revenue, your accountant.

## 5. National Insurance (NIB)

- Rates since 1 July 2024: **employer 6.65%, employee 4.65%, total 11.3%** of wages, up to the ceiling of **B$810 a week** (B$3,510 a month, B$42,120 a year). New rates were expected from 1 July 2026; check.
- Register as an employer with NIB before your first employee. Monthly returns and payments are due by the 15th. Late penalty reported at 5% a month.
- Self-employed persons must also contribute. Check your own self-employed rate with NIB.
- **Industrial benefits** cover employees injured at work: injury benefit (up to 40 weeks, 3 waiting days), medical care, disablement. The employer files form B44 (Employer's Report on Accident). Claims within 3 months. NIB states employers who pay contributions are excused from liability for job injuries and diseases.
- **Subs:** their workers are their responsibility, so require an NIB letter of good standing from every sub before they start. An uninsured sub's injured worker becomes your problem.
- **Letter of good standing** for yourself is needed for government tenders and many corporate clients.

**Confirm with:** National Insurance Board.

## 6. Labour and immigration

- Minimum wage: **B$260 a week, B$6.50 an hour**, since January 2023 (confirm no later change).
- Overtime reported at 1.5 times after 40 hours a week or 8 hours a day; public holidays and rest days at 2 times. Employment Act, 2001 governs. Give written terms of employment.
- **Employee or sub?** If you control how and when they work, supply the tools and pay by the hour, they are probably your employee, and NIB applies. A true sub has their own business licence, own tools, own crew, quotes a price for a scope.
- **Immigration.** Non-Bahamians need a work permit. Employing someone without permission is an offence. Check your own employees and require subs to certify their crews are legal. Spot-check.
- Health and Safety at Work Act applies to sites. Run the safety system in `05-field-playbook.md`.

**Confirm with:** Department of Labour, Department of Immigration, NIB.

## 7. Permits and inspections

**New Providence and most Family Islands:**
1. **Town Planning** (Department of Physical Planning): zoning, use, setbacks, subdivision approval where needed.
2. **Building permit** from **Building Control**, Ministry of Public Works. Required for new construction, additions and alterations, including decks, sheds, retaining walls and fences. Plans reviewed for structure, safety and code compliance. Plans by a qualified architect or engineer for anything structural.
3. **Inspections** at stages. Typical: footings before pour, slab before pour, tie beam before pour, roof framing and straps, electrical rough, plumbing rough, final.
4. **Certificate of occupancy** and utility sign-offs before power and water connection.

**Freeport:** Grand Bahama Port Authority Building and Development Services handles permits (Civic Industrial Area, Cedar Street; 242-350-9154 or 9166). The City of Freeport says contractor licences are mandatory for work within city limits and that work not done by the property owner must be done by a licensed contractor.

**Timing.** An older benchmark put a Building Control permit at about 120 days. Incomplete drawings and unanswered queries are the main cause of delay. Submit complete packages and answer queries the same day. Never start before the permit.

**Confirm with:** Ministry of Public Works Building Control, Department of Physical Planning, GBPA.

## 8. Building code

- The **Bahamas Building Code, 3rd edition (2003)** with amendments still governs private work. It was patterned on ASCE 7 loads, raised design wind speeds, and made hurricane shutters mandatory. The design wind speed is widely cited as 150 mph.
- On 30 September 2025 Cabinet approved new standards only for public works and coastal defences and ordered the existing amendments consolidated. A full 4th edition was still pending at last report.
- Get a copy of the code and amendments from the Ministry of Public Works. Keep it in the truck.

**Confirm with:** Ministry of Public Works.

## 9. Government work: Public Procurement Act, 2023

- Replaced the 2021 Act in 2023, partly because the 2021 rules favoured large vendors.
- Allows preferences for small and medium businesses, women-owned, youth-owned and Family Island businesses. The margin is set in each tender document. The government also pledged 5% of procurement to businesses owned by young Bahamians (a campaign pledge, not a statutory figure).
- Register on the government eProcurement portal and supplier registry (Ministry of Finance / Public Procurement Department).
- Typical documents: business licence, Tax Compliance Certificate, NIB letter of good standing, company documents, insurance, references.
- Reality: government pays slowly. Price the wait, keep reserves, and keep government under about a third of your work until you are strong.

**Confirm with:** Public Procurement Department, Ministry of Finance.

## 10. Funding for a young Bahamian business

- **Access Accelerator** (government small business agency, formerly SBDC): standalone grants reported up to B$5,000, loan guarantees with partner banks (75% to 90% guarantees on some programmes), youth programmes. Rounds open and close; check what is live.
- **Bahamas Development Bank:** concessional loans reported at 0% to 2% for the first two years, up to about B$100,000, for MSMEs including youth-owned (2024 SDG programme with UN Women). Check current terms.
- **OPM Young Entrepreneurs Academy:** training and funding for youth.
- Rule: borrow for equipment that earns its payments, not to cover losses.

## 11. Taxes you do not pay, and ones you do

- No personal income tax. No corporate income tax for a domestic business like yours. (A 15% top-up tax applies only to very large multinational groups.)
- You do pay: VAT (once registered), business licence tax, NIB, customs duty and VAT on imports, real property tax on property you own, stamp duty on property transfers and some documents.

## 12. Getting paid when a client won't pay

- **The Bahamas has no mechanics' lien law.** A contractor cannot put a lien on the property for non-payment. You enforce the contract: sue in the courts (Supreme Court for larger claims) or arbitrate if the contract says so.
- So your protection is the contract: deposit, milestone billing, right to stop work, title to materials stays with you until paid, interest on late payment, and attorney's fees. See `03-contracts.md`.
- Arbitration: The Bahamas has an Arbitration Act (2009). Name arbitration in Nassau in your contracts for larger jobs. For small debts, a demand letter from an attorney often works; your attorney advises on court choice.
