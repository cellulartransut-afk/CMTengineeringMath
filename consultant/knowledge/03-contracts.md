# 03. Contracts

As of 2026-11-03. Bahamian law is English common law plus Bahamian statutes. These are working rules from the field. Have a Bahamian attorney review your two master templates once (owner contract and subcontract); after that, adapt them per job and only send unusual terms back to the attorney.

## First principles

A contract does three jobs:
1. **Defines the product.** What exactly you will build, to what standard, and what you will not do.
2. **Allocates risk.** Who pays when something goes wrong: hidden conditions, weather, price rises, delays, defects.
3. **Controls money.** When, how much, and what happens when payment is late.

Everything you leave out gets decided later by whoever has the money, which is the client. Write it down.

Because The Bahamas has no lien law, the contract is your only security. Deposits, milestone billing, a right to stop work and title to materials are how you get paid.

## Pick the right contract type

| Type | You get paid | You carry | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lump sum (fixed price) | One agreed price | Cost overrun risk | Clear scope with drawings or a tight written scope. Most of your work. |
| Time and materials (T&M) with a cap | Labour at agreed rates + materials at cost + markup, up to a cap | Less risk; client pays for unknowns | Repairs where you cannot see the problem until you open it up (leaks, rot, termite damage). |
| Cost plus fee | Actual cost + fixed fee or % | Low cost risk, but you must prove every cost | Trusted repeat clients, fast-track work. Open books. |
| Cost plus with guaranteed maximum price (GMP) | Cost + fee up to a ceiling; savings shared | Overruns above GMP | Larger jobs with good clients, later stages. |
| Unit price | Rate per unit x measured quantity | Productivity risk | Block laying, site work, roads, repetitive work. |
| Design-build | One price for design and construction | Design risk too | Only with a registered architect or engineer under you and professional indemnity insurance. Later stage. |

## Standard forms you will meet

- **JCT** (UK) and **FIDIC** (international): used on larger Bahamian, resort and government projects. They fit English-style law well.
- **AIA** (US): used by US developers and architects.
- Big clients' own forms: usually written for the client. Read every clause.

Rule: never sign a standard form with "amendments" you have not read. The amendments are where the risk was moved to you.

## The clause checklist for every contract you issue

| # | Clause | What it must say | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parties | Full legal names, addresses, company numbers | You sue a legal person, not a nickname |
| 2 | Site | Address, lot number, island | |
| 3 | Scope | What is included, line by line, tied to drawings or a written scope | Scope you did not write is scope you do free |
| 4 | Exclusions | What is not included (list below) | Stops arguments before they start |
| 5 | Documents and priority | Contract, scope, drawings, specs; which governs if they conflict | |
| 6 | Price and VAT | Price, whether VAT is included or added, currency B$ | |
| 7 | Payment schedule | Deposit, milestones, due in X days | Cash flow |
| 8 | Late payment | Interest per month on overdue sums, right to suspend work after written notice | No lien law; this is your lever |
| 9 | Title to materials | Materials stay your property until paid | Lets you remove unincorporated materials if unpaid |
| 10 | Changes | No change without a signed change order with price and time | |
| 11 | Time | Start date, duration, what extends time | |
| 12 | Extensions of time | Weather, hurricanes and named storms, owner delays, late selections, permit and inspection delays, shipping and customs delays, unforeseen conditions | You will not control these |
| 13 | Owner duties | Access, water, power, permits (who pulls them), decisions within X days, selections by dates | |
| 14 | Unforeseen conditions | Hidden rot, termite damage, rock, buried services: priced as a change | |
| 15 | Price escalation | For long jobs or volatile items: right to adjust if a supplier price rises more than X% after signing | Steel, lumber, freight move fast |
| 16 | Insurance | What you carry; owner insures the existing structure | |
| 17 | Warranty | Workmanship 12 months from completion (defects liability period); manufacturer warranties passed through; excludes misuse, storms, owner's other contractors | Defines your exposure |
| 18 | Retention | If the client holds retention: max 5% to 10%, released at completion or end of the defects period | |
| 19 | Termination | Both sides; what is paid on termination | |
| 20 | Disputes | Talk first, then mediation, then arbitration in Nassau (or courts for small sums) | |
| 21 | Governing law | Laws of the Commonwealth of The Bahamas | |
| 22 | Notices | How and to whom; WhatsApp and email accepted | |
| 23 | Entire agreement and signatures | All terms are in the document; signed and dated by both | |

## Standard exclusions list (put the relevant ones in every quote)

- Permit, plan review and inspection fees, unless stated
- Architectural or engineering drawings, surveys, soil tests
- Utility connection, meter and deposit fees (BPL, Water and Sewerage, cable)
- Rock excavation, removal of buried debris, dewatering
- Repair of hidden damage found after opening up: rot, termites, mould, corroded steel, defective existing wiring or plumbing
- Hazardous materials (asbestos, lead paint)
- Moving or protecting furniture and contents
- Landscaping and reinstatement beyond the work area
- Owner-supplied items: their delivery, defects and warranty
- Security, after-hours and weekend work
- Hurricane damage during construction (covered by insurance, not by the price)

## Change orders

1. Client asks, or you find a condition.
2. You write the change order: description, price, time added, signed.
3. Client signs. Then you do the work.
4. Emergency (safety, water coming in): do the minimum to make safe, notify in writing the same day, price within 48 hours.

Pricing a change: same unit costs and markup as the base estimate, plus a fair fee for disruption. Never "we'll sort it out at the end". At the end, you have no leverage.

## Subcontracts: protect yourself and treat subs right

Your subcontract must flow down the main contract: the sub is bound to you as you are bound to the client.

| Clause | What it says |
|---|---|
| Scope | Their exact scope, with drawings; what you supply; what they supply |
| Price | Lump sum or unit rate; VAT if they are registered |
| Payment | For work done and accepted; paid within X days of you being paid by the client for that work |
| Retention | 10% held until their work is accepted and warranties delivered; release at completion or end of defects period |
| Documents before start | Business licence, NIB letter of good standing, public liability certificate naming you, list of crew |
| Labour law | They pay their workers, NIB, and certify every worker is legally allowed to work in The Bahamas |
| Schedule | Start, duration, crew size; they follow your site schedule |
| Quality | Work to drawings, code and manufacturer instructions; you can reject defective work; they fix at their cost |
| Safety | PPE, your site rules, toolbox talks; you can remove anyone unsafe |
| Clean up | Daily; or you clean and backcharge |
| Backcharges | Written notice, 48 hours to fix, then you fix and deduct |
| No direct dealing | They don't take instructions, payments or side work from your client on this job, and don't solicit your client for 12 months |
| Warranty | 12 months, same as yours to the client |
| Indemnity | They cover claims from their work and their people |
| Termination | For default after written notice; you can finish their work and deduct the extra cost |

**Pay-when-paid.** The UK outlawed it by statute; The Bahamas has no equivalent statute. Under the common law it is generally a matter of the clause's wording, and courts read these clauses strictly. Write it clearly, and get your attorney to settle the wording. Ethically and practically: when the client pays you for a sub's work, pay that sub within days. Your bench is your business.

## Red flags in contracts other people give you

- Uncapped liquidated damages (daily penalty for delay). Cap at 5% to 10% of the price and tie to real loss.
- Pay-if-paid with no time limit: you might never get paid.
- "No damages for delay": you absorb the client's delays.
- Scope written as "all work necessary for a complete project": you carry design gaps.
- Client can withhold payment "at its sole discretion".
- Unlimited indemnity, including the client's own negligence.
- Design responsibility on you without a design fee or engineer.
- Retention over 10% or with no release date.
- Waiver of claims if you don't notify within 2 or 3 days.
- Owner-supplied materials with no rules on lateness or defects.

Ask for these to be changed in writing. If they won't change, price the risk or walk away.

## Verbal deals and WhatsApp

A verbal contract can be valid, but you can't prove it. If someone gives you an instruction on site or by phone, confirm it the same day in writing ("As discussed today, you asked us to ... Price B$... Time ... days. Please confirm."). A WhatsApp or email trail beats memory every time. A signed document beats both.
