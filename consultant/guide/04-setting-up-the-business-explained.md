# 04. Setting up the business, explained step by step

Rev 1 · 2026-11-04

## What this guide is

A walk-through, in order, of everything you register, open and buy to become a legal Bahamian contracting business. For each step: what it is in plain words, why you need it, what to bring, what to ask, what you walk out with, and who confirms it.

**Where the facts come from.** Every rate, fee and rule here is from [`consultant/knowledge/02-bahamas-rules.md`](../knowledge/02-bahamas-rules.md), **as of 2026-11-03**. Rates and laws change. Where a detail (a form, a fee, a document list, a processing time) is not in that file, this guide does not guess. It gives you the exact question to ask the office. Write down every answer you get: date, office, the person's name, what they said.

**Three rules for every office visit.**
1. Call or check the office's website first and ask what to bring. Carry your government photo ID and copies of anything you've already been issued.
2. Keep a "business documents" folder, paper and scanned. Every certificate, licence, registration and letter goes in it the day you receive it.
3. Never pay anyone "something extra" to speed things up. It is a bribe, it is illegal, and in a country this size it follows you for life. If something is slow, ask politely, in writing, what is missing.

Words you don't know: [`01-glossary.md`](01-glossary.md).

## Step 1. Choose: sole trader or company

### What it is

| Option | What it is in plain words | Use when (from `02`) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole trader with a registered business name** | You trade under a business name, but you and the business are the same legal person. Simple. | Only for the first few very small jobs. |
| **Company limited by shares** (Companies Act) | The company is a separate legal person. It signs contracts, owns things and owes debts in its own name. Protects your personal assets from most business debts and claims. Banks will still want your personal guarantee on loans. | Before any job over about B$25,000, before hiring employees, before government work. Recommended early, because construction liability is high. |

### Why it matters

Think about what happens when something goes wrong.

**Example.** Your plumber's fitting fails and floods the client's house. The client claims B$30,000.
- **Sole trader:** the claim is against you personally. Your savings, your vehicle and anything you own are exposed.
- **Company:** the claim is against the company. Your personal assets are protected from most claims like this. (Your attorney will explain the exceptions, and any loan you personally guaranteed stays your personal debt.)

You will also be signing contracts with clients, subs and suppliers worth thousands of dollars. A company keeps those obligations in the company. Later (Stage 4 in your roadmap) you will use separate companies for contracting, real estate and equipment, so a disaster in one does not take down the others.

### What it costs

- Company formation is usually done through a Bahamian attorney or registered agent. Costs vary by agent. Packages including government fees have been quoted around **B$1,000 to B$2,000**. Get two quotes.
- There is an **annual government fee** to keep the company in good standing. Ask the amount and due date.
- A sole trader business name registration costs less, but ask the Registrar General's Department or the agent for the current fee.

### Your age

At 18 you are an adult and can sign contracts and be a company director. Your attorney confirms this when incorporating.

### What to ask the attorney or registered agent

Book a meeting (your roadmap says do this in week 1). Ask:
1. "For a one-person contracting business planning to grow, would you recommend a company now or later? Why?"
2. "What is your total price to incorporate, including government fees? What exactly is included?"
3. "What is the annual government fee, when is it due, and what do you charge each year to keep the company in good standing?"
4. "What documents will I receive, and which ones will the bank and Inland Revenue need?"
5. "Do I need a registered office or ongoing registered agent? A company secretary? What do those cost?"
6. "If I trade under a name different from the company name, do I need to register that name separately?"
7. "How long does incorporation take?"
8. "Can you also review my two contract templates (owner contract and subcontract) once, and what would that cost?"

### Decision

Unless the cost stops you completely, set up the company before you sign anything bigger than a small repair. You are in a high-liability trade and you plan to grow. If you start as a sole trader, set a trigger in writing: "Incorporate before the first job over B$25,000, the first employee, or the first government bid, whichever comes first."

**Confirm with:** Registrar General's Department, a Bahamian attorney.

## Step 2. Register the business name or the company

### What it is

The **Registrar General's Department** is the government office that registers business names and companies. Registration is usually done through a Bahamian attorney or registered agent, who files the papers for you.

### Why you need it

It creates the legal identity that everything else hangs on. The bank account, the business licence, your contracts, your insurance and your NIB registration all need the registered name or company.

### How to do it

1. **Pick a name, and two backups.** Make it simple, easy to spell, and not too close to an existing contractor's name. Check that the matching web address and social media handles are free too.
2. **Check the name is free at the Registrar General.** Your roadmap says week 1. Ask the agent to check, or ask the Registrar General's Department how to search.
3. **Instruct the attorney or agent** (from Step 1) to register the business name or incorporate the company.
4. **Give them what they ask for.** Ask for their list in writing before the meeting. Expect to be asked for your identification and the details of the directors and shareholders. For you, that may be just you.

### What to ask

- "Is the name [ ] available? If not, which of my backups is?"
- "What exactly will I receive when registration is complete?"
- "How do I prove the company exists when the bank or a client asks? Do I need certified copies?"
- "What filings must I make every year, and when?"

### What you walk out with

Whatever the agent confirms. You need proof of the business's or company's legal existence, which the bank and Inland Revenue will ask for. Scan it into your business documents folder the same day. Write the company number (if a company) into your profile and onto your contract templates; the owner contract template has a space for it.

**Confirm with:** Registrar General's Department, your attorney or registered agent.

## Step 3. Business licence (Department of Inland Revenue)

### What it is

Every business needs a business licence to trade. Under the **Business Licence Act, 2023** (in force from 1 July 2023), you apply for and renew it with the **Department of Inland Revenue** (DIR).

The business licence **tax** is charged on your **gross turnover of the previous year** (all the money you took in from sales, before costs), not on profit. "Turnover" includes rent and royalties.

Reported bands (from law-firm summaries of the Bill; confirm against the Second Schedule of the Act):

| Prior-year turnover | Reported rate |
|---|---|
| Up to B$100,000 | Exempt from the tax. **You still need the licence.** |
| Above B$100,000 to B$500,000 | 0.5% |
| Above B$500,000 to B$5M | 0.75% |
| Above B$5M | 1.25% (audited statements required above B$5M) |

**Freeport:** to trade in the Port area you also need a **Grand Bahama Port Authority** business licence.

### Why you need it

- Today you can trade as a contractor with a business licence. It is the legal permission to do business.
- Clients, subs and suppliers will ask for your licence number. Your quote, contract and invoice templates all have a space for it.
- Government tenders ask for it.
- The planned Construction Contractors Board's proposed requirements include a valid business licence.

### Example (illustration)

Your first year's turnover is B$80,000. That is under B$100,000, so in year 2 you are reported exempt from the tax, but you still renew the licence.

Your second year's turnover is B$180,000. If the 0.5% rate applied to the whole amount, the year-3 tax would be 180,000 x 0.5% = B$900. Ask DIR whether the rate applies to the whole turnover or only to the part above each band. Reserve the money monthly in your tax account (B$900 / 12 = B$75 a month).

### What to bring

Ask DIR before you go. Expect to need your registration documents from Step 2 and your ID.

### What to ask DIR

1. "What do I need to submit to apply for a business licence for a new contracting business?"
2. "Can I apply online, or do I come in? Which office?"
3. "Is there an application fee for the licence itself?"
4. "As a brand-new business with no prior-year turnover, what do I declare in my first year?"
5. "When is the renewal due each year, and what do I submit at renewal?"
6. "Are the tax bands applied to the whole turnover or only the portion within each band?"
7. "What records of turnover should I keep for renewal?"
8. "How should the licence be displayed or quoted on my documents?"

### What you walk out with

A business licence and its number. Put the number on your quote, contract and invoice templates, and in `my-business/profile.md`.

**Confirm with:** Department of Inland Revenue. In Freeport, also the Grand Bahama Port Authority.

## Step 4. Business bank account

### What it is

A bank account in the business's name, used only for business money. (`02` doesn't list bank requirements; the rule comes from `06-money.md`.)

### Why you need it

- **Every business cent in and out goes through it. Never mix personal and business money.** If you pay groceries from the job account, or client deposits land in your personal account, you will never know whether a job made money, and your books will be a mess for the accountant, the bank and Inland Revenue.
- Your bank history becomes your credit history. Banks lend to businesses whose accounts they can read.
- You need a second account, the **tax account**, where you move VAT collected, NIB and a business licence reserve every time you are paid. That money is not yours.

### What to bring

Ask the bank for its list before you go. Expect to be asked for your ID and your business registration or company documents (Step 2), and possibly the business licence (Step 3). Ask whether the licence must come first.

### What to ask the bank

1. "What documents do you need to open a business account for a [company / sole trader with a registered business name]?"
2. "Do I need the business licence before opening, or can I open with the registration documents?"
3. "What are the monthly fees, transaction fees and minimum balance?"
4. "Can I open a second account in the business name for taxes?"
5. "Online banking: can I make and receive transfers, and download statements for my accounting software?"
6. "How can clients pay me: transfer, cheque, card?"
7. "Who can sign on the account?"
8. "Do you have programmes for young or small businesses? Are you a partner bank for Access Accelerator loan guarantees?"
9. "Who would be my contact person for the business account?"

### What you walk out with

A business operating account and a business tax account, online banking, and the name of a contact at the bank. Start the relationship now. In Stage 2 you will want a bank facility, and the banker who has watched your account for two years is the one who says yes.

**Confirm with:** the bank.

## Step 5. National Insurance (NIB)

### What it is

NIB is the National Insurance Board, the Bahamian social security system. Contributions are paid on wages. Two registrations matter to you:
- **You, as self-employed.** Self-employed persons must contribute. Check your own self-employed rate with NIB.
- **You, as an employer.** Register as an employer with NIB **before your first employee**.

Rates since 1 July 2024 (new rates were expected from 1 July 2026; check):

| | Rate | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Employer | 6.65% | Wages up to the ceiling |
| Employee | 4.65% (deducted from their pay) | Wages up to the ceiling |
| **Total** | **11.3%** | |
| Ceiling | B$810 a week (B$3,510 a month, B$42,120 a year) | |

- Monthly returns and payments are due **by the 15th**. Late penalty reported at **5% a month**.
- **Industrial benefits** cover employees injured at work: injury benefit (up to 40 weeks, 3 waiting days), medical care, disablement. The employer files **form B44** (Employer's Report on Accident). Claims within 3 months. NIB states employers who pay contributions are excused from liability for job injuries and diseases.
- A **letter of good standing** from NIB (proof you are up to date) is needed for government tenders and many corporate clients.

### Why you need it

- It is the law, for you as self-employed and for anyone you employ.
- It protects you: an employee injured at work is covered by NIB industrial benefits if you contributed for them.
- Your clients will ask for your letter of good standing, exactly as you ask your subs for theirs.

### Example (illustration, at the rates above)

You hire a labourer at B$400 a week.

| | Calculation | B$ a week |
|---|---|---|
| Employer share (your cost) | 400 x 6.65% | 26.60 |
| Employee share (deducted from pay) | 400 x 4.65% | 18.60 |
| **Total paid to NIB** | | **45.20** |
| Labourer's take-home before other deductions | 400 - 18.60 | 381.40 |
| Your total cost for the labourer | 400 + 26.60 | 426.60 |

So the true cost of a B$400-a-week worker is B$426.60 a week, before anything else. Price it in. Check the minimum wage too: **B$260 a week, B$6.50 an hour**, since January 2023 (confirm no later change).

### Employee or sub? Get this right before you pay anyone

If you control how and when they work, supply the tools and pay by the hour, they are probably your **employee**, and NIB applies. A true **sub** has their own business licence, own tools, own crew, and quotes a price for a scope. Calling an employee a "sub" does not change what they are.

### What to ask NIB

1. "How do I register and contribute as self-employed? What is my rate, and how and when do I pay?"
2. "If I own a company and pay myself from it, do I contribute as self-employed or as an employee of my company?"
3. "How do I register as an employer? What do I bring? How long does it take?"
4. "How do I file the monthly return and pay by the 15th?"
5. "Did new contribution rates take effect from 1 July 2026? What are they?"
6. "How do I get a letter of good standing, how long does it take, and how long is it valid?"
7. "Where do I get form B44, and how do I file it if an employee is injured?"

### What you walk out with

Your self-employed registration and, when you hire, an **NIB employer number** (the subcontract template has a space for a sub's NIB employer number; you'll have one too). Later, letters of good standing whenever a tender or client asks.

**Confirm with:** National Insurance Board. Employment questions (minimum wage, overtime, written terms): Department of Labour. Work permits: Department of Immigration.

## Step 6. VAT (when and how)

### What it is

Value Added Tax. Standard rate **10%** (cut from 12% in 2022). Construction services are standard-rated.

- **Mandatory registration** once your taxable supplies (your sales) pass **B$100,000 in any 12 months**.
- You **can register voluntarily** below that.
- **Registered:** you charge 10% on your invoices (output VAT), file returns, and claim back VAT you paid on materials and subs (input VAT), subject to the rules.
- **Not registered:** you cannot charge VAT, and the VAT you pay on materials is a cost. Price it in.

### Why it matters

**Example: how VAT works when you are registered.**

| | B$ |
|---|---|
| Your invoice to the client | 20,000 |
| Output VAT you charge, 10% | 2,000 |
| Client pays you | 22,000 |
| Input VAT you paid on materials and subs | 900 |
| **VAT you owe Inland Revenue** (2,000 - 900) | **1,100** |

**Should you register early?** From `02`:
- **Yes**, if most of your clients are businesses or government. They want VAT invoices and can claim the VAT back, so it costs them nothing extra.
- **Maybe not**, if most of your clients are homeowners. Your price looks 10% higher than an unregistered competitor's. Remember, though, that the unregistered competitor pays VAT on their materials and can't claim it back, so the real gap is smaller than 10%. Run both versions for your typical job with your accountant.

**When must you register? Track it monthly.**
Rolling 12-month example: your sales for the 12 months to the end of January are B$92,000. In February you invoice B$15,000. Last February you invoiced B$5,000, which drops out of the window. New 12-month total = 92,000 - 5,000 + 15,000 = **B$102,000**. You have passed B$100,000. Register.

**Two more VAT points for your clients and bigger jobs.**
- **2025 change for big projects.** The government moved to restrict input VAT claims on "major construction" (reported as projects of B$1M or more, and work like dredging, reclamation, docks and marinas), except where the business supplies real property as a taxable activity. Check the enacted text before pricing any job near B$1M.
- **First-time homeowners.** A VAT rebate on construction services and materials for first-time homeowners exists (Section 55A, VAT Act; up to B$40,000 under the 2022 measures). The 2026/27 budget proposed extending first-time homeowner relief to duplexes through fourplexes with one owner-occupied unit, and raising the real property tax exemption from B$500,000 to B$600,000. Tell qualifying clients; it helps them say yes.

### What to ask Inland Revenue (and your accountant)

1. "How do I register for VAT? Online or in person? What do I submit?"
2. "Once my turnover passes B$100,000 in 12 months, how quickly must I register?"
3. "If I register voluntarily now, what are my filing obligations?"
4. "How often will I file returns, and when are they due?"
5. "What must appear on a VAT invoice?"
6. "Can I claim input VAT on purchases made before my registration date?"
7. "What is my TIN (tax identification number), and where do I use it?" (Your quote and contract templates have a VAT TIN field.)
8. "How does the first-time homeowner rebate work for my clients, and what paperwork do they need?"

### What you walk out with

When you register: confirmation of VAT registration and your TIN, which goes on every quote and invoice. Until then: a monthly rolling-turnover check (checklist 7.4 in [`02-checklists-explained.md`](02-checklists-explained.md)) and "not VAT registered" on your documents.

**Confirm with:** Department of Inland Revenue, your accountant.

## Step 7. Public liability insurance

### What it is

Insurance that pays claims for injury or damage to other people caused by your work: the neighbour's car, a passer-by hit by a falling block, the client's flooded floor. (Insurance types come from `06-money.md`; `02` doesn't cover insurance.)

### Why you need it

- One accident on one job can be bigger than a year of profit.
- Clients ask for it. Your owner contract (clause 11.1) promises a certificate on request.
- You require it from every sub, so you should carry it yourself.

**Timing.** `06-money.md` says public liability goes in place **before your first job**. Your roadmap lists getting quotes in weeks 8 to 12, but first jobs can start in week 4. Follow the stricter rule: get quotes in weeks 2 to 4 and have the policy before your first job.

### The other covers you will meet

| Cover | What it does | When |
|---|---|---|
| Public liability (general liability) | Injury or damage to third parties from your work | Before your first job |
| Contractor's all risks (CAR) | Damage to the works during construction: fire, theft, storm | Each job, or an annual policy. Check named-storm cover. |
| Motor | Your vehicles | Always |
| Tools and equipment | Theft and damage | When the kit is worth it |
| Employees | NIB industrial benefits cover work injuries for employees who are contributed for | When you hire |
| Professional indemnity | Design errors | Only if you do design-build |

### How to get quotes

1. Get names of at least **two Bahamian insurance brokers**. Ask contractors you know and the BCA who they use.
2. Call each and describe your business plainly: "New contracting company in [island]. Residential repairs and renovations, B$[ ] to B$[ ] per job. I manage subcontractors; no employees yet. Expected turnover year 1 about B$[ ]."
3. Ask the questions below. Get the answers and the quote in writing.
4. Compare like for like (the same way you level sub quotes): limit, deductible, exclusions, price.

### What to ask each broker

1. "What liability limits do small contractors here typically carry, and what limits do bigger clients and government usually ask for?"
2. "What is the deductible?"
3. "What is excluded? Roofing? Water damage? Work at height? Hurricane and named storm?"
4. "Does the policy cover work done by my subcontractors, or only my own employees?"
5. "Does it cover work on the Family Islands?"
6. "Can you issue a certificate naming a client as additional insured? How quickly?"
7. "What is the premium, and can it be paid monthly?"
8. "What would CAR cover cost per job, and as an annual policy? Does it include named storms, and what is the named-storm deductible?"
9. "Do you offer tools and equipment cover?"
10. "How do I make a claim, and what is the notice period?"

### What you walk out with

A policy schedule (the summary page of what is covered) and a **certificate of insurance** you can send to clients. Put the policy number and renewal date in `my-business/profile.md` and your calendar.

**Confirm with:** your insurance broker. Your attorney checks that the insurance clauses in your contracts match what your policy actually covers.

## Step 8. Join the Bahamian Contractors Association (BCA)

### What it is

The trade association for Bahamian contractors. It has pushed for years to bring the Construction Contractors Act into force, including a threatened judicial review in 2024 and a petition in February 2026. Under the planned Construction Contractors Board, it nominates six of the ten members.

### Why you need it

- **Credibility.** The BCA itself calls the market a "free for all" until licensing starts. Membership tells clients you are serious.
- **Network.** Other contractors, subs, suppliers and broker recommendations. People to learn from.
- **Early news on licensing.** The Board had not been appointed as of April 2026 reporting. The BCA reported the grandfathering window for existing contractors runs about 2026 to 2028, starting when the Board is announced. Proposed licensing requirements include proof of a completed project in your category, a valid business licence and no outstanding complaints. You want to hear the day it opens.

Your roadmap puts joining in weeks 8 to 12.

### What to ask the BCA

1. "What membership categories do you have, and which one fits a new small contractor?"
2. "What is the membership fee, and what are the requirements to join?"
3. "Do you have a student or young contractor programme?"
4. "What meetings, training or mentoring do you run?"
5. "Will you help members prepare for licensing and grandfathering when the Board opens? What records should I be keeping now?"
6. "Can members use the BCA name or logo on their documents?"

### What you walk out with

Membership, a list of upcoming meetings, and contacts. Go to the meetings. The membership is worth little if nobody knows your face.

**Confirm with:** the BCA. Licensing status: Ministry of Public Works, the BCA, the Official Gazette.

### Start your licensing file now

This belongs with the BCA step because it is what the Board is expected to ask for. For **every** job, from job one, keep:
- the contract
- the permit (if any)
- photos before, during and after
- the final invoice
- proof of payment
- a letter or testimonial from the client

When the Board opens, you apply with evidence, not stories.

## Step 9. Government supplier registration and the Tax Compliance Certificate

### What it is

**Government work** is bought under the **Public Procurement Act, 2023**. It replaced the 2021 Act, partly because the 2021 rules favoured large vendors. To bid, you register on the government **eProcurement portal and supplier registry** (Ministry of Finance / Public Procurement Department).

The Act allows **preferences** for small and medium businesses, women-owned, youth-owned and Family Island businesses. The margin is set in each tender document. The government also pledged 5% of procurement to businesses owned by young Bahamians; that was a campaign pledge, not a figure in the law.

Typical documents a tender asks for: business licence, **Tax Compliance Certificate**, NIB letter of good standing, company documents, insurance, references.

The **Tax Compliance Certificate** is, in plain words, a document showing your business is up to date with its taxes. `02` lists it as a typical tender document but does not say who issues it or how; ask Inland Revenue first, since it is the tax office.

### Why you need it, and when

- As a young Bahamian small business, the preferences are written with you in mind.
- **But government pays slowly.** Price the wait, keep reserves, and keep government under about a third of your work until you are strong. Never let one ministry cheque decide your payroll.
- Your roadmap puts government work in **Stage 2** (year 2 to 5). Registering earlier lets you see what tenders look like and what they ask for, so you can build toward them.

### What to ask the Public Procurement Department

1. "How do I register on the eProcurement portal and supplier registry? What documents must I upload?"
2. "Is there a registration fee?"
3. "How do I claim the youth-owned or small business preference? What proof of ownership and age do you need?"
4. "Where are small works tenders advertised, and what size of job do they usually cover?"
5. "Do tenders for small works ask for bid bonds or performance bonds?"
6. "How long do government payments usually take after an invoice is approved?"

### What to ask Inland Revenue about the Tax Compliance Certificate

1. "Do you issue the Tax Compliance Certificate? If not, who does?"
2. "What must be up to date before I can get one: business licence, VAT, anything else?"
3. "How do I apply, and how long does it take?"
4. "How long is it valid?"

### What you walk out with

A supplier registration on the portal, and, when needed, a current Tax Compliance Certificate and NIB letter of good standing. Keep the expiry dates in your calendar; tenders close fast and you won't have time to chase documents.

**Confirm with:** Public Procurement Department, Ministry of Finance. Tax Compliance Certificate: Department of Inland Revenue.

## Step 10. Access Accelerator and the Bahamas Development Bank

### What they are

| Programme | What it is (as reported in `02`) |
|---|---|
| **Access Accelerator** | The government small business agency (formerly SBDC). Standalone grants reported up to **B$5,000**. Loan guarantees with partner banks (**75% to 90%** guarantees on some programmes). Youth programmes. Rounds open and close. |
| **Bahamas Development Bank (BDB)** | Concessional loans reported at **0% to 2% for the first two years**, up to about **B$100,000**, for small businesses including youth-owned (2024 programme with UN Women). |
| **OPM Young Entrepreneurs Academy** | Training and funding for young entrepreneurs. |

### Why they matter

A loan guarantee means the government agency promises the bank it will cover most of the loss if you can't repay. That makes a bank far more willing to lend to an 18-year-old with no track record. A 0% to 2% loan is much cheaper money than an ordinary bank loan.

### The rule before you borrow

**Borrow for equipment that earns its payments, not to cover losses.** If a job is losing money, a loan only makes the loss bigger. Fix the pricing.

**Test any equipment loan** with the payback formula from `06-money.md`: payback months = purchase price / (monthly rental income or savings - monthly running, maintenance and insurance). Under 24 months with steady demand is a good buy.

Example (illustration): a used mixer costs B$4,800. Renting one costs you B$400 a month on jobs, and owning it costs B$100 a month to run and insure. Saving = B$300 a month. Payback = 4,800 / 300 = 16 months. Good buy, if you really use it every month.

### Be ready before you apply

Programmes like these usually want to see a real business. Have these in your folder first:
- registration documents (Step 2), business licence (Step 3), business bank statements (Step 4)
- a short business plan with numbers (use the money math in `01-roadmap.md` and `consultant/tools/estimator.xlsx`)
- your job log (`my-business/jobs.md`) and licensing file

### What to ask Access Accelerator, BDB and the OPM programme

1. "Which programmes are open right now, and when do they close?"
2. "Who is eligible? Is there a minimum age, minimum time in business, or turnover requirement?"
3. "Is this a grant (no repayment) or a loan? If a loan, what is the interest rate after the first two years, and what is the term?"
4. "What collateral or personal guarantee do you need?"
5. "What documents and business plan do you need with the application?"
6. "Which banks are partner banks for the loan guarantees?"
7. "How long from application to decision, and from approval to money?"
8. "Is there training or mentoring attached?"

### What you walk out with

A clear list of what is open, what you qualify for, and the documents to prepare. Apply only when you have a specific use that passes the payback test.

**Confirm with:** Access Accelerator, Bahamas Development Bank, the Office of the Prime Minister (Young Entrepreneurs Academy).

## Before you hire your first employee

Do these before anyone works for you on wages (all from `02`):
1. **Register with NIB as an employer** (Step 5).
2. **Pay at least minimum wage:** B$260 a week, B$6.50 an hour, since January 2023 (confirm no later change).
3. **Overtime** reported at 1.5 times after 40 hours a week or 8 hours a day; public holidays and rest days at 2 times. The Employment Act, 2001 governs.
4. **Give written terms of employment.** Ask the Department of Labour or your attorney what they must contain.
5. **Check they are legally allowed to work.** Non-Bahamians need a work permit. Employing someone without permission is an offence.
6. **Safety:** the Health and Safety at Work Act applies to sites. Run the safety system in `05-field-playbook.md`.

**Confirm with:** Department of Labour, Department of Immigration, NIB.

## What you pay, and what you don't

From `02`, section 11:
- **You don't pay:** personal income tax, or corporate income tax for a domestic business like yours. (A 15% top-up tax applies only to very large multinational groups.)
- **You do pay:** VAT (once registered), business licence tax, NIB, customs duty and VAT on imports, real property tax on property you own, stamp duty on property transfers and some documents.

## One page: order of operations

Print this. Tick as you go. Write the date next to each tick.

**Week 1**
- [ ] Fill in `consultant/my-business/profile.md` (cash, vehicle, island, monthly costs).
- [ ] Pick a business name and two backups. Check social handles and web address.
- [ ] Meet a Bahamian attorney or registered agent. Decide sole trader or company. Get two quotes. (Step 1)
- [ ] Ask the Registrar General (or agent) to check the name is free. (Step 2)

**Weeks 1 to 2**
- [ ] Register the business name or incorporate the company. Scan the documents. (Step 2)
- [ ] Apply for the business licence at Inland Revenue. In Freeport, the GBPA licence too. (Step 3)
- [ ] Open the business operating account and the business tax account. (Step 4)
- [ ] Register with NIB as self-employed. Ask how it works for a company owner. (Step 5)

**Weeks 2 to 4**
- [ ] Business phone with WhatsApp Business, business email, Google Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram.
- [ ] Set up accounting software. Load cost codes (`consultant/knowledge/07-cost-codes.md`).
- [ ] Get two public liability quotes. **Buy the policy before your first job.** (Step 7)
- [ ] Start the licensing file folder: contract, permit, photos, invoice, proof of payment, client letter for every job.
- [ ] Start collecting sub documents for your bench (`my-business/subs.md`).
- [ ] Have an attorney review your owner contract and subcontract templates once.

**Before your first employee**
- [ ] NIB employer registration. (Step 5)
- [ ] Written terms of employment; minimum wage; overtime rules; work status checked.

**Weeks 8 to 12**
- [ ] Join the BCA. Go to a meeting. (Step 8)

**Every month, from the first invoice**
- [ ] Rolling 12-month turnover checked against B$100,000. Register for VAT on time, or earlier if most clients are businesses or government. (Step 6)
- [ ] VAT, NIB and business licence reserves moved to the tax account.
- [ ] NIB return and payment by the 15th (once you are an employer).

**Every year**
- [ ] Renew the business licence with Inland Revenue.
- [ ] Pay the company's annual government fee (if a company).
- [ ] Renew insurance; send updated certificates to anyone who needs them.
- [ ] Accountant prepares financial statements. Reviewed statements by year 2 or 3.

**Stage 2, or earlier to learn how tenders work**
- [ ] NIB letter of good standing and Tax Compliance Certificate. (Steps 5 and 9)
- [ ] Register on the eProcurement portal and supplier registry. (Step 9)

**Only when you have a specific use that passes the payback test**
- [ ] Apply to Access Accelerator, BDB or the OPM programme. (Step 10)

**Every six months**
- [ ] Ask the consultant to check every rate and law in `02-bahamas-rules.md` against current sources.
