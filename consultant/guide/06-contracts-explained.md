# 06. Contracts explained: every contract and form, clause by clause

Rev 1 · 2026-11-04

## How to use this

- This guide covers every contract and form in `../templates/`. For each one it explains what it's for, what every clause says in plain words, why the clause protects you, what it costs the client, and how to fill the form in using a worked example. It ends with the three mistakes people make with it.
- Look up any word you don't know in the [glossary](01-glossary.md). Links below go to the right letter.
- Names, addresses and prices in the examples are made up for practice. They are **not** price guidance. Your prices come from your own estimate (`../tools/estimator.xlsx`) and written quotes from your subs and suppliers.
- Clause numbers match the templates at Rev 1 · 2026-11-04. If a template's Rev line changes, check the numbers again.
- Your attorney has not reviewed the templates yet. Get that done once before your first real job ([contract-guide.md, section 6](../templates/contract-guide.md#6-what-a-bahamian-attorney-must-check)).
- Companion guides: [00-start-here.md](00-start-here.md), [01-glossary.md](01-glossary.md), [05-spreadsheets-explained.md](05-spreadsheets-explained.md).

Three words used everywhere:
- **You**: the contractor, your business.
- **Client** or **owner**: the person or company paying you.
- **Sub**: a [subcontractor](01-glossary.md#s), a separate business you hire for part of the work.

## First principles: what a contract is for

A contract does three jobs:
1. **Defines the product.** Exactly what you will build, and what you won't.
2. **Shares out the risk.** Who pays when something goes wrong: hidden rot, a storm, a late shipment, a defect.
3. **Controls the money.** When you get paid, how much, and what happens when payment is late.

Anything the contract doesn't say gets decided later by whoever is holding the money. That's the client.

The Bahamas has no mechanics' [lien](01-glossary.md#l) law. If a client doesn't pay, you can't put a claim on their house. The contract is your only security. That is why every contract you use has a deposit, stage payments, interest, the right to stop work and title to materials.

**Be honest about the cost.** Every clause that protects you costs the client something: paying earlier, deciding sooner, giving up some claims. Know what each clause costs them and say it plainly. Clients sign when they understand a contract, not when you hide what's in it. Every clause below has a "Costs the client" line so you can explain it straight.

## 1. Short-Form Job Agreement

Template: [short-form-job-agreement.md](../templates/short-form-job-agreement.md)

**What it is:** a two-page fixed-price contract. Page 1 describes the job. Page 2 is 19 short standard terms.
**Use it when** the job is under about B$10,000 and you can see everything you are pricing. If the problem is hidden, use the T&M agreement. If there are drawings, structural work or a permit for an alteration, use the small works agreement.

**Words:** [lump sum](01-glossary.md#l), [scope of work](01-glossary.md#s), [exclusions](01-glossary.md#e), [deposit](01-glossary.md#d), [milestone billing](01-glossary.md#m), [change order](01-glossary.md#c), [unforeseen conditions](01-glossary.md#u), [extension of time](01-glossary.md#e), [title retention](01-glossary.md#t), [right to suspend](01-glossary.md#r), [warranty](01-glossary.md#w), [punch list](01-glossary.md#p).

### Page 1, box by box

**Parties table**
- *Says:* who the contract is between: legal names, licence number, VAT status, address, phone, email.
- *Protects you:* you can only sue a legal person, and only if you have the name right. "Mr. Ferguson from Golden Gates" is not a party. "Sandra Ferguson, of [address]" is.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Site, and the owner's consent line**
- *Says:* where the work is. If the person hiring you doesn't own the property, they confirm the owner has agreed in writing.
- *Protects you:* a tenant hires you to replace a door. The landlord comes by, says nobody asked him, stops the job and won't pay. The consent line makes you check first.
- *Costs the client:* one phone call to the owner.

**Work included**
- *Says:* the job, line by line: where, how much, what material, what standard.
- *Protects you:* every job argument starts with "I thought that was included". Five tight lines settle it before it starts.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. They get a clear list of what they're paying for.

**Materials, Permit, Not included**
- *Says:* who supplies materials, any allowance, who gets the permit, and what you are not doing.
- *Protects you:* "Not included" is where you write down what clients assume is included: hidden damage, painting beyond the work area, moving furniture.
- *Costs the client:* items on that list cost extra if they come up, but the client knows that before signing.

**Price**
- *Says:* the price, the VAT line, the total.
- *Protects you:* one agreed number. If you aren't VAT registered, write "not charged" and charge no VAT. Charging VAT without being registered is illegal. Not charging it once you are registered means the VAT comes out of your profit.
- *Costs the client:* the price.

**Payment table**
- *Says:* a deposit on signing, then a progress payment and a completion payment, each due within [3] days of your invoice.
- *Protects you:* the deposit pays for the materials before you spend anything. The progress payment keeps the money ahead of the work. Without them you buy the doors with your own money and hope.
- *Costs the client:* paying part of the price before they see work. That's why you give a receipt for every dollar and show the supplier invoices.

**Time**
- *Says:* a start date, or a start after the deposit clears; the duration; working hours.
- *Protects you:* the clock starts when you have the money, not when they sign.
- *Costs the client:* the start depends on how fast they pay.

**Special conditions**
- *Says:* anything agreed for this job only. It overrides page 2.
- *Protects you:* anything you negotiate goes in here in writing. Nobody scribbles over the standard terms.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Signatures**
- *Says:* the owner confirms they read both pages and got a copy.
- *Protects you:* "I never saw page 2" is answered by their signature on page 1 and their initials on page 2.
- *Costs the client:* two minutes.

### Page 2, term by term

**Term 1. Scope**
- *Says:* you do only the page 1 work. Anything else needs a change order.
- *Protects you:* you quote to fix the fascia, and the owner points at the back porch "while you're here". Without term 1, "while you're here" becomes free work.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. Extras get priced instead of assumed.

**Term 2. Payment**
- *Says:* the client pays by the page 1 schedule. You order and start only after the deposit clears.
- *Protects you:* you buy B$900 of doors and lumber on a promise. The house sale falls through and the owner says "hold off". Now you own two doors that fit nobody's frames.
- *Costs the client:* money before work starts.

**Term 3. Late payment**
- *Says:* overdue sums carry [1.5]% interest a month. The client also pays your reasonable costs of collecting, including legal costs.
- *Protects you:* without interest, paying you late is free, so you get paid last, after BPL and the school fees.
- *Costs the client:* nothing if they pay on time. B$1,155 paid one month late costs 1,155 x 1.5% = B$17.33. Collection costs apply only if they make you hire an attorney.

**Term 4. Right to stop work**
- *Says:* if a payment is [7] days overdue, you may stop work after [3] days' written notice. The finish date moves, and the client pays your costs of stopping and restarting.
- *Protects you:* this is your only real lever without a lien law. Say the client misses the progress payment and says "I'll pay it all at the end". Without term 4, stopping work would put *you* in breach. With it, you stop legally, after notice (letters 6 and 7).
- *Costs the client:* their own job stops. Restart costs only arise if they caused the stop.

**Term 5. Materials stay ours until paid**
- *Says:* materials delivered to site stay yours until paid for. If the client doesn't pay, you can collect anything not yet built in.
- *Protects you:* two prehung doors sitting in their carport are yours until paid for, not a gift.
- *Costs the client:* nothing if they pay.
- *Watch out:* take back only materials not built in, with photos and a witness, never by force. If they object, stop and call your attorney.

**Term 6. Changes**
- *Says:* no extra work on verbal instructions. A change counts only when its price and time are agreed in writing before the work: a change order signed by both of you, or, for changes under B$[500], your WhatsApp stating the change, price and time plus their written approval.
- *Protects you:* the owner asks your plumber on site to "fix the outside tap too". He does it and bills you B$120. The owner says it was included. Term 6 gives you a rule to point to, and your subs know it.
- *Costs the client:* they have to decide on extras and agree a price first. Small ones take one WhatsApp.

**Term 7. Hidden conditions**
- *Says:* if you find something that couldn't be seen or expected when you priced, you stop that part, send photos and price the fix. The fix is extra. In an emergency you make safe first, tell them the same day and price it within 48 hours.
- *Protects you:* you pull off the rotten fascia and the rafter tails behind it are termite-eaten. Without term 7, new rafters are inside your fixed price.
- *Costs the client:* they pay for real problems in their own house, which are there whether you find them or not. They see photos and a price before they decide.

**Term 8. Time extensions**
- *Says:* the finish date moves fairly for weather, storms and hurricanes (including watches, warnings and clean-up), owner delays, permit and inspection delays, shipping, customs and supplier delays, hidden conditions, and anything else outside your control. You notify the client in writing within [7] days.
- *Protects you:* a tropical storm watch in September closes the site for three days. The doors sit at the wharf on a customs hold. Without term 8 you're "late", and the owner holds back the final payment.
- *Costs the client:* the finish date can move. They get written notice when it does.

**Term 9. Your part (the client's duties)**
- *Says:* give access; [provide water and power]; clear contents from the work area; disclose known problems; don't instruct or pay your workers directly. Anything the client supplies is their risk, and you warrant only your installation.
- *Protects you:* the client buys a cheap door online and it arrives warped. You hang it, it sticks, and they blame you. Term 9 says the door is theirs to answer for.
- *Costs the client:* a few duties they'd expect anyway.

**Term 10. Standards**
- *Says:* good and workmanlike work to the Bahamas Building Code and manufacturers' instructions. Licensed electricians and plumbers. You may use subs and remain responsible for them.
- *Protects you:* a client asks your carpenter to "just run a wire" for a new light. Unlicensed electrical work fails inspection and can burn a house down.
- *Costs the client:* licensed trades cost more. That's the law, and it's their safety.

**Term 11. Warranty**
- *Says:* 12 months on your workmanship from completion. Manufacturers' warranties pass to the client. Not covered: wear, misuse, storms, others' work, items the client supplied, existing parts you didn't touch.
- *Protects you:* eighteen months later the door sticks because the house settled, or a hurricane rips the fascia off. Without limits, every problem forever is "your work".
- *Costs the client:* nothing. They get a year of free fixes for your mistakes. After that, repairs are paid.

**Term 12. Limit of liability**
- *Says:* your total liability is capped at the price. You are not liable for loss of rent, profit or use, or other indirect loss. The cap doesn't apply to death or injury caused by your negligence, or to fraud.
- *Protects you:* the house sale slips a week, the buyer walks, and the seller says you owe her B$5,000. On a B$3,850 job, one claim like that wipes out more than the job's profit.
- *Costs the client:* they can't claim more than the price from you, and can't claim lost rent or a lost sale. Their own insurance covers big losses. Injury and fraud are not capped.

**Term 13. Insurance**
- *Says:* you carry public liability insurance. The client keeps the building and contents insured, including windstorm.
- *Protects you:* a fire spreads from a neighbour's yard and burns the house while you're working. The client's insurer pays for the house. Yours pays only if you caused the damage.
- *Costs the client:* keeping the policy they should have anyway.

**Term 14. Completion**
- *Says:* you tell them when you're done, and you walk the job together within [3] days. Punch list items are fixed within [7] working days. The punch list doesn't hold up the completion payment, except for a set holdback.
- *Protects you:* without it, a client can hold back the whole final payment for a month over one paint touch-up.
- *Costs the client:* they can hold back only the stated small sum.

**Term 15. Ending the agreement**
- *Says:* either side may end it for a serious breach that isn't put right within [7] days of written notice. If it ends for any reason other than your fault, the client pays for work done, materials ordered and your costs of leaving. If it ends through your fault, the client pays for work properly done, less the extra cost of finishing.
- *Protects you:* the owner's buyer decides to do the work himself, and the owner cancels halfway. You've already bought the doors. Term 15 says who pays for them.
- *Costs the client:* cancelling costs them what's done and ordered. If you fail, they are protected too.

**Term 16. Disputes**
- *Says:* meet within [7] days. If it isn't settled in [14] days, go [to mediation, then] to the courts of The Bahamas. Either side can still claim a sum that is due and not disputed.
- *Protects you:* a set route stops a dispute dragging on. The last sentence stops a client holding the whole bill over B$100 of it.
- *Costs the client:* a meeting.

**Term 17. Law**
- *Says:* Bahamian law governs.
- *Protects you:* the client's son in Florida can't insist on Florida law.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Term 18. Notices and photos**
- *Says:* notices go in writing. Email or WhatsApp to the page 1 contacts counts. Photos are for your records, and for marketing only with consent and without the address.
- *Protects you:* "You never told me" is answered by the WhatsApp.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. Their address stays private.

**Term 19. Whole agreement**
- *Says:* page 1, these terms, the special conditions and signed change orders are the whole deal. Earlier quotes and conversations don't count.
- *Protects you:* "You said on the phone you'd paint the whole house." If it isn't written down, it isn't the deal.
- *Costs the client:* if they want a promise to count, they get it written in. Same protection for them.

### How to fill it in: worked example (pre-sale repair, B$3,850)

**The job.** Sandra Ferguson is selling her house at Lot 14, [street], Golden Gates, New Providence. The buyer's home inspection listed repairs, and closing is three weeks away. Your estimate gives a price of B$3,850. You are not VAT registered.

| Blank | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement no. | Your job number (SOP-15) | 2026-004 |
| Date | The day you both sign | [date] |
| Contractor legal name | Exactly as registered with the Registrar General | [Your registered business name] |
| Company no. or registered business name | Company number once incorporated; otherwise the registered business name | [ ] |
| Business licence no. / VAT | Your licence number; "not VAT registered" | [your no.] / not VAT registered |
| Owner legal name | As on the conveyance | Sandra Ferguson |
| Address, phone and WhatsApp, email | Both sides, complete | [ ] |
| Site | Lot, street, area, island | Lot 14, [street], Golden Gates, New Providence |
| Owner consent line | Fill it only if the client isn't the owner | N/A: client is the owner |
| Work line 1 | Where, how much, what, what standard | Fascia: remove and replace 16 ft of rotten fascia board on the front elevation with primed board; prime and paint 2 coats to match |
| Work line 2 | | Kitchen sink: replace leaking P-trap and both supply lines; licensed plumber; test for leaks |
| Work line 3 | | Interior doors: supply and install 2 prehung 30-inch hollow-core doors, bedrooms 2 and 3, with passage sets; paint white |
| Work line 4 | | Shutters: re-fasten loose accordion shutter tracks at 3 front windows with new stainless steel fasteners; test operation |
| Work line 5 | Keep it | Clean-up: daily tidy; our debris removed at the end |
| Materials | | We supply all materials |
| Permit | | Not required: like-for-like repairs (if in doubt, ask Building Control) |
| Not included | The things she'll assume | Hidden damage behind the fascia (rafter tails, roof deck); items on the buyer's inspection report not listed above; painting beyond the repaired areas; permit fees; moving contents |
| Price / VAT / Total | | 3,850.00 / not charged: we are not VAT registered, 0.00 / 3,850.00 |
| Payment | Stages must add up to 100% | Deposit 40% = 1,540.00 on signing. Progress 30% = 1,155.00 when the doors and fascia are installed. Completion 30% = 1,155.00. Due within 3 days of invoice. |
| Pay by | | Bank transfer to [bank, account name, account no.] |
| Time | | Start within 3 working days after the deposit clears. Duration about 4 working days. 7:00 am to 6:00 pm, Monday to Saturday. |
| Special conditions | Use it for the closing date | "The Owner's closing is [date]. We aim to finish by [date, at least 5 working days before]. Term 8 still applies." |
| Page 2 brackets | Keep or change each one on purpose | Interest 1.5%; 7 days and 3 days (term 4); B$500 for WhatsApp changes; 7 days (term 8); [provide water and electricity,] kept; walk 3 days, punch list 7 working days, holdback B$150; 7 days (term 15); delete "[refer it to mediation in Nassau and then]" for a job this size |
| Signatures | | She signs. You sign with your title. Witness optional. Both of you initial page 2. |

The cash test: 1,540 + 1,155 + 1,155 = 3,850. The deposit must cover the doors, fascia board, paint and fasteners plus your first day. If it doesn't, raise it or have her pay the supplier directly.

### The 3 mistakes people make with it

1. **A payment column that doesn't add up.** The defaults are [40], [30] and [30]. If you change one, change another so they still total 100%, and check the B$ column adds up to the price.
2. **Loose scope lines.** "Fix doors" invites a fight. "Supply and install 2 prehung 30-inch hollow-core doors, bedrooms 2 and 3, with passage sets, painted white" doesn't.
3. **Wrong form, or a hard deadline promised.** Don't use the short form for hidden conditions, structural work, a permit job, or anything over about B$10,000. Don't promise a closing date in conversation: write the target date in Special conditions and say term 8 still applies.

## 2. Small Works Construction Agreement

Template: [owner-contract-small-works.md](../templates/owner-contract-small-works.md)

**What it is:** your full fixed-price contract: 17 clauses plus Schedule A (scope) and Schedule B (exclusions).
**Use it for** jobs from about B$10,000 to B$250,000, and for any job with drawings, a permit, structural work or several subs. Over about B$250,000 you need a JCT or FIDIC form chosen and amended by your attorney.

**Words:** [documents and priority](01-glossary.md#d), [allowance](01-glossary.md#a), [extension of time](01-glossary.md#e), [liquidated damages](01-glossary.md#l), [escalation clause](01-glossary.md#e), [workmanlike](01-glossary.md#w), [CAR insurance](01-glossary.md#c), [owner-supplied items](01-glossary.md#o), [practical completion](01-glossary.md#p), [defects liability period](01-glossary.md#d), [termination](01-glossary.md#t), [mediation](01-glossary.md#m), [arbitration](01-glossary.md#a), [governing law](01-glossary.md#g), [entire agreement](01-glossary.md#e), [notice](01-glossary.md#n), [Schedule A and Schedule B](01-glossary.md#s).

Several clauses are the same idea as a short-form term. Where that's the case, the note says so; read the short-form explanation above for the full reasoning.

### Clause by clause

**Parties (top of the agreement)**
- *Says:* full legal names, company number or registered business name, licence number, VAT status, contacts.
- *Protects you:* same as the short-form parties table. If two people own the house, name both and have both sign.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**1. The Site**
- *Says:* address, lot number, subdivision, island.
- *Protects you:* in a new subdivision houses have no numbers yet. "The yellow house by the church" is not a site. A lot number is.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**2. The Works**
- *Says:* 2.1 the job is Schedule A. 2.2 lists the documents that form the contract and which one wins in a conflict: this agreement, then Schedule A, then the drawings, then the specs, then your proposal. 2.3 anything not in Schedule A is excluded, and Schedule B lists the main exclusions.
- *Protects you:* the sketch shows a 3 x 5 ft shower and the proposal says 3 x 4. Clause 2.2 decides which one counts. Without it, the client picks whichever suits them.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**3. Contract Price**
- *Says:* 3.1 the price and VAT status. 3.2 the price changes only by signed change order, unforeseen conditions (8) or escalation (9). 3.3 (optional) allowances: a set sum for items not yet chosen. If the client's choice costs more or less, the difference plus your fee goes on a change order.
- *Protects you:* the client can't knock money off because "it looked easy", and you can't add money without the paperwork either. Allowance example: the vanity allowance is B$650, and Mrs. Rolle falls for a B$1,100 vanity. The change order is (1,100 - 650) x 1.15 = B$517.50. Without 3.3 you either refuse her vanity or eat B$450.
- *Costs the client:* they pay more if they choose dearer, less if they choose cheaper.

**4. Payment**
- *Says:* 4.1 the payment table. 4.2 interest on late payment. 4.3 right to suspend. 4.4 title to materials. 4.5 the client pays your legal costs of recovering overdue sums. 4.6 how to pay, and no cash to workers or subs.
- *Protects you:* 4.1 to 4.4 work the same as short-form terms 2 to 5. Test 4.1 on the estimator's Cash Flow sheet: your cash position must stay positive at every stage. 4.5 matters because an attorney's letter and a court case can eat a big share of a small debt; without 4.5 you never get that back. 4.6 matters because if the client pays your tile man B$500 cash "to speed things up" and takes it off your invoice, nobody can prove what it was for.
- *Costs the client:* money ahead of the work. Interest only if late: B$2,843.75 paid one month late costs 2,843.75 x 1.5% = B$42.66. Legal costs only if they make you chase. Every payment gets a receipt.

**5. Time**
- *Says:* 5.1 the start date, or [ ] days after the latest of: deposit cleared, permit issued, access given. 5.2 completion in [working days](01-glossary.md#w). 5.3 extensions of time for the six listed causes (a) to (f), with your written notice within [7] days. 5.4 (optional) delay damages, capped at [5]%, as the client's only remedy for delay.
- *Protects you:* 5.1 means the clock starts only when you can actually work. 5.3 works like short-form term 8, but with lettered causes so your delay letter can name one: the plumbing inspection booked for Tuesday happens on Friday, so that's 3 days under 5.3(c). 5.4 exists for clients who insist on a penalty: it is capped and runs only after extensions. Delete it unless they ask.
- *Costs the client:* the finish date can move, with notice. If 5.4 is used, it gives *them* money for a late finish: for example B$100 a week, capped at 5% of B$11,375 = B$568.75.

**6. Owner's Responsibilities**
- *Says:* (a) access and working hours; (b) water and power; (c) who gets and pays for the permit; (d) decisions and selections on time, with late selections extending time; (e) clear the contents; (f) no dealing directly with your subs or workers; (g) disclose known defects, hazards and past damage.
- *Protects you:* (d): Mrs. Rolle can't choose her tile for two weeks, so the tile sub moves to another job. Late selections extend time. (g): the owners knew the old shower had leaked into the wall for a year and didn't say. That's disclosure they owed you.
- *Costs the client:* decisions on time, an empty room, a few utilities.

**7. Changes**
- *Says:* 7.1 no change is valid without a change order signed by both of you before the work. 7.2 emergency work to make safe comes first, with notice the same day and a change order within 48 hours. 7.3 changes are priced at your estimate rates, or at cost plus [ ]%.
- *Protects you:* same idea as short-form term 6, but stricter: every change needs a signed change order, with no WhatsApp exception unless you add one as a special condition. 7.2 example: you open the wall and a corroded supply pipe bursts. You shut it off and cap it now, then notify and price it. 7.3 settles in advance how changes are priced.
- *Costs the client:* decisions in writing. They know your markup up front.

**8. Unforeseen Conditions**
- *Says:* conditions you couldn't see or expect are notified, the affected work stops if needed, and the fix is priced as a change.
- *Protects you:* same as short-form term 7. Rolle example: with the old tub out, the floor under it was rotted from an old leak. That became CO-01, B$520 and 1 working day.
- *Costs the client:* they pay for real problems in their house, with photos and a price first.

**9. Price Escalation (optional)**
- *Says:* on jobs over [8] weeks or with volatile materials, if a named material's supplier price rises more than [5]% between signing and purchase, the price changes by the actual increase, shown on invoices.
- *Protects you:* you price a roof in March. By June, after a storm somewhere in the region, lumber and freight have jumped. On a four-month job that can wipe out your margin.
- *Costs the client:* they carry real price rises over 5%, with proof. Delete the clause on short jobs where your quotes are locked.

**10. Standards, Permits and Inspections**
- *Says:* 10.1 good and workmanlike work to the drawings, the code and manufacturers' instructions. 10.2 licensed electrical and plumbing work. 10.3 you arrange the inspections the permit requires.
- *Protects you:* it sets the quality bar to a standard, not to taste. "Perfect grout lines" is not a standard. Workmanlike work to the tile maker's instructions is.
- *Costs the client:* licensed trades and inspections take money and time.

**11. Insurance**
- *Says:* 11.1 your public liability insurance, with a certificate on request. 11.2 the client insures the building and contents, including windstorm. 11.3 (optional) works insurance ([CAR](01-glossary.md#c)), including named windstorm.
- *Protects you:* a pipe your plumber never touched bursts and floods the hallway: the client's insurer handles it. If your plumber caused it, your public liability does. On new builds and additions, 11.3 decides who insures the half-built work against a storm.
- *Costs the client:* keeping their policy, and carrying CAR if they agree to.

**12. Warranty**
- *Says:* 12 months on workmanship from practical completion (the Defects Period). Manufacturers' warranties pass to the client. Exclusions as in short-form term 11.
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same as short-form term 11.

**13. Owner-Supplied Items**
- *Says:* items the client supplies are their responsibility for delivery, suitability and defects. You warrant the installation, not the item. If they arrive late, time extends.
- *Protects you:* the Rolles buy the toilet in Florida and it arrives with a cracked tank. You install the replacement whenever it arrives, the finish date moves, and the toilet itself is their problem.
- *Costs the client:* the risk on what they buy.

**14. Practical Completion and Handover**
- *Says:* 14.1 the job is practically complete when it's finished except minor items that don't stop use. You walk it together within [3] days of your notice and agree a punch list. 14.2 punch list items are done within [10] working days, and they don't hold up the completion payment except for a set holdback.
- *Protects you:* without it, "we're not paying until everything is perfect" means the final payment and the defects period never start. Use letter 9 and Form 4.
- *Costs the client:* they can hold back only the set amount. Write a fixed B$ figure, not a %, so nobody argues which price it's a percentage of.

**15. Termination**
- *Says:* 15.1 either side may terminate for a material breach not fixed within [10] days of written notice. 15.2 if it ends for any reason other than your breach, the client pays for work done, materials and demobilisation, plus [10]% of the unfinished balance as lost overhead. 15.3 if it ends for your breach, the client pays for work properly done, less the extra cost of finishing.
- *Protects you:* halfway through, the Rolles decide to sell and cancel. Work done is B$6,000 of B$11,375, so the unfinished balance is B$5,375. Under 15.2 they pay for the work, materials and demobilisation, plus 10% x 5,375 = B$537.50. That covers the overhead you carried for the job you turned other work away for.
- *Costs the client:* cancelling costs them 10% of what's left. You can drop that on small jobs (contract-guide section 3). Your attorney checks it isn't a penalty.

**16. Disputes**
- *Says:* 16.1 meet within [7] days. 16.2 mediation in Nassau if not settled in [14] days. 16.3 then arbitration in Nassau or the courts (pick one, delete the other). 16.4 undisputed sums can always be claimed.
- *Protects you:* a set route, and 16.4 stops a client holding the whole bill over a small disagreement. For a job this size the courts are usually simpler. Arbitration suits bigger jobs.
- *Costs the client:* time to meet and mediate.

**17. General**
- *Says:* 17.1 Bahamian law. 17.2 entire agreement. 17.3 notices in writing, by email or WhatsApp to the listed contacts; a notice sent before 5 pm on a business day counts that day, otherwise the next business day. 17.4 photos. 17.5 you may subcontract and stay responsible. 17.6 limit of liability: capped at [the Contract Price / B$ ], no loss of rent, profit or use, except death, injury from negligence, or fraud.
- *Protects you:* 17.3: a notice sent at 7 pm on a Friday counts from Monday, so count your 3 days under 4.3 from Monday. 17.5: the client can't say "I hired you, not your tile man", and they shouldn't, because you are responsible. 17.6 works like short-form term 12.
- *Costs the client:* same as the matching short-form terms.

**Schedule A (Scope) and Schedule B (Exclusions)**
- *Says:* the job line by line, and what's excluded.
- *Protects you:* this is where jobs are won or lost. Copy them word for word from the accepted proposal.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

## 3. Time and Materials Repair Agreement

Template: [time-and-materials-repair-agreement.md](../templates/time-and-materials-repair-agreement.md)

**What it is:** a repair contract where the client pays your actual time at agreed rates, plus materials at cost plus a markup, up to a cap the client controls.
**Use it when** you can't see the problem until you open up: leaks, rot, termite damage, hidden plumbing, storm make-safe.

**Words:** [time and materials](01-glossary.md#t), [markup](01-glossary.md#m), [landed cost](01-glossary.md#l), [unforeseen conditions](01-glossary.md#u), [deposit](01-glossary.md#d), [demobilisation](01-glossary.md#d).

**First principle.** A fixed price on hidden work is a bet, and the house always wins. On T&M you give up the extra profit you'd make if the job turned out easy, and in exchange you can't lose on the unknown. The client gives up price certainty and gets control through the cap. That trade is fair, and it's why clients sign it.

### Clause by clause

**Key numbers box**
- *Says:* cap, emergency pre-approval, deposit, markup, invoice terms, rates.
- *Protects you:* the numbers that matter are on page 1, so nobody can say they missed them.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. It's transparency.

**1. The Problem and the Work**
- *Says:* 1.1 the problem in the client's words. 1.2 you investigate, open up and repair it, and the first steps are listed. 1.3 why this is T&M, and that any estimate is a guide, not a quote. 1.4 what's not included.
- *Protects you:* the client calls about "a leak". You fix the vent flashing. Two weeks later a window on another wall leaks and they say you didn't fix "the leak". 1.1 records which problem you took on.
- *Costs the client:* they pay actual time instead of a fixed price, and accept that the estimate may be wrong.

**2. Rates**
- *Says:* 2.1 labour and equipment at Schedule 1 rates. 2.2 materials at cost plus [ ]%. Cost includes delivery, freight, duty, brokerage, and VAT you can't claim back. Receipts are attached. 2.3 subs, skips and dump fees at invoice plus [ ]%.
- *Protects you:* the markup pays for your time sourcing, buying, hauling and handling. Without it you do the shopping for free.
- *Costs the client:* the markup. In return they see every receipt.

**3. Not-to-Exceed Cap**
- *Says:* 3.1 you can't charge more than the cap without written approval, and WhatsApp or email counts. 3.2 the cap is a spending limit, not a fixed price. 3.3 you warn the client at [75]%. 3.4 at the cap you stop safely, notify the same day with Form B, and restart only on written approval. 3.5 an emergency make-safe amount above the cap, approved by signing the agreement. 3.6 if there's no reply in [2] working days, you can leave site, and coming back is a new call-out. 3.7 if the client won't raise the cap, the work ends under 15.3.
- *Protects you:* this is what gets clients to sign T&M, because they stay in control. It protects you because every dollar above the cap is approved in writing, so nobody can call the bill a surprise.
- *Costs the client:* they have to answer quickly. If they don't, the job stops.

**4. Deposit**
- *Says:* 4.1 a deposit on signing. 4.2 it's held to the final invoice (or applied to the first), and any unused balance is refunded within [7] days.
- *Protects you:* a retainer held to the end is your security on the last bill.
- *Costs the client:* money held, refunded if not used.

**5. Timesheets, Photos and Records**
- *Says:* 5.1 a daily timesheet (Form A). 5.2 sent [daily / weekly]. 5.3 dated photos at every stage. 5.4 the client queries a timesheet within [3] working days or it's accepted, and a query doesn't hold up the rest of the invoice.
- *Protects you:* T&M fights are about hours. Daily timesheets with photos end the fight before it starts.
- *Costs the client:* reading the timesheets within 3 days.

**6. Extra Work**
- *Says:* nothing outside clause 1 without written approval. Extra work counts toward the cap. No verbal instructions.
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same idea as short-form term 6.

**7. Invoices and Payment**
- *Says:* 7.1 weekly invoices with timesheets and receipts. 7.2 due in [5] days. 7.3 interest and recovery costs. 7.4 right to suspend. 7.5 title to materials. 7.6 payment method.
- *Protects you:* weekly billing keeps the money close behind the work. Never let a T&M job run four weeks unbilled. 7.3 to 7.5 work like short-form terms 3 to 5.
- *Costs the client:* a bill every week, which also means no shock at the end.

**8. Hidden Conditions and Existing Defects**
- *Says:* 8.1 finding and fixing hidden problems *is* the work, inside the cap. 8.2 if it needs an engineer, a permit or a much bigger job, you stop and advise, and the client pays those fees. 8.3 you're not responsible for damage that was there before.
- *Protects you:* you open the ceiling and find a truss with a split bottom chord. Stop: that's an engineer's call. 8.3 means that by finding it you haven't taken on the whole roof.
- *Costs the client:* engineer and permit fees.

**9. Time and Completion**
- *Says:* 9.1 you start on a date and work with reasonable speed, with no fixed end date. 9.2 you're not responsible for delays outside your control. 9.3 at the end you notify, send photos and the final invoice, and the client may inspect within [3] days.
- *Protects you:* no completion date means no "you're late" claim on a job nobody could size.
- *Costs the client:* no promised finish date.

**10. Owner's Responsibilities**
- *Says:* (a) to (g): access; utilities; clear contents; disclose known problems; reply to approvals within [1] working day, or [2] hours in an emergency; arrange tenant access; no direct dealing.
- *Protects you:* T&M stalls when the owner goes quiet. Item (e) puts a time on it.
- *Costs the client:* staying reachable.

**11. Standards and Permits**
- *Says:* workmanlike work; licensed trades; permit if needed, and no permit-requiring work before it's issued, except make-safe.
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same idea as small works clause 10.

**12. Warranty**
- *Says:* 12.1 12 months on the repair you did. 12.2 if the client chooses a patch over your recommended full repair, you note it in writing and the warranty on that item drops to [30 days / none]. 12.3 temporary work carries no warranty. 12.4 no warranty on the rest of the existing system. 12.5 exclusions.
- *Protects you:* you recommend re-roofing the slope; the owner wants a B$300 patch. When shingles blow off two slopes over, 12.2 and 12.4 say that isn't your warranty.
- *Costs the client:* they choose between a cheap patch with a short warranty and a full repair with 12 months.

**13. Limit of Liability**
- *Says:* liability is capped at what you've been paid [or B$ ]. No loss of rent, bookings, profit or use. Death, injury from negligence and fraud are not capped.
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same as short-form term 12.

**14. Insurance**
- *Says:* your public liability; the client insures the building and contents. The client makes their own insurance claim, and you provide photos and a report.
- *Protects you:* leak repairs often feed an insurance claim. 14.2 makes clear it's their claim, and your photos help them.
- *Costs the client:* nothing new.

**15. Ending the Agreement**
- *Says:* 15.1 either side may end it on [2] days' notice. 15.2 you may end it if an invoice is [14] days overdue or the site is unsafe. 15.3 the client pays up to the end date, plus non-returnable materials and the cost of making safe.
- *Protects you:* an easy exit both ways makes T&M easy to sign, and lets you walk away from a bad client fast.
- *Costs the client:* paying for everything up to the end date.

**16. Disputes and Law, 17. General**
- *Says:* talk first, then [mediation and] the courts; Bahamian law; notices by email and WhatsApp; entire agreement; subcontracting; photos.
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same as short-form terms 16 to 19.

**Schedule 1, Form A, Form B**
- *Says:* the rate card; the daily timesheet; the approval-to-exceed-cap form.
- *Protects you:* rates agreed before the work, hours recorded the same day, cap raised in writing.

## 4. Maintenance Service Agreement

Template: [maintenance-service-agreement.md](../templates/maintenance-service-agreement.md)

**What it is:** a 12-month service contract. The client pays a monthly fee in advance for scheduled inspections and a set number of hours of minor work. Everything else goes through a Work Order at agreed rates.
**Use it for** landlords, property managers, gated communities and short-term rental owners, especially owners living abroad who need a trustworthy local. This is recurring income, one of the Stage 1 businesses in your [roadmap](../knowledge/01-roadmap.md).

**Words:** [warranty](01-glossary.md#w), [notice](01-glossary.md#n), [hurricane watch / warning](01-glossary.md#h), [price gouging](01-glossary.md#p), [cistern](01-glossary.md#c), [GFCI](01-glossary.md#g), [termites](01-glossary.md#t), [B$](01-glossary.md#b).

### Clause by clause

**Parties, the authority line and the local representative**
- *Says:* who the client is. A property manager confirms they have the owner's authority. A local representative may approve Work Orders up to B$[ ].
- *Protects you:* a property manager signs for an owner in Toronto. Later the owner says the manager had no right to spend. The authority line puts that on the manager. The local representative covers the 2 am water heater burst while the owner is asleep in London.
- *Costs the client:* naming someone local they trust.

**Key numbers box**
- *Says:* start date, term, fee, included hours, spending limits, hurricane option.
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same idea as the T&M key numbers.

**1. Properties and Term**
- *Says:* 1.1 the properties are in Schedule 1. 1.2 12 months. 1.3 it renews automatically unless either side gives 30 days' notice before the end. 1.4 you may change the fee for a renewal with [60] days' notice.
- *Protects you:* recurring income without selling the contract again every year, and a fee review once a year.
- *Costs the client:* a year's commitment (with an early exit in 15.2), and 30 days' notice to leave at renewal.

**2. Included Services**
- *Says:* 2.1 inspections, minor items up to the included hours, filters, reports, a baseline inspection. 2.2 "minor items" means about [1] hour each with common materials, with examples. 2.3 licensed electrical and plumbing work is outside the hours. 2.4 unused hours don't carry over [or carry one month], and extra hours are charged. 2.5 materials at cost plus [ ]%.
- *Protects you:* the owner expects the monthly fee to cover repainting the unit between tenants. 2.2 says minor means about an hour.
- *Costs the client:* paying extra for anything bigger.

**3. Fees and Payment**
- *Says:* 3.1 fees in advance by the [1st], with the first month on signing. 3.2 Work Orders due in [7] days. 3.3 B$, with US$ accepted at par, and the client pays the bank and wire fees. 3.4 VAT status. 3.5 payment method.
- *Protects you:* an owner in Atlanta wires US$550 for the month, and the two banks take their fees, so less than B$550 arrives. 3.3 says she covers the fees so you get the full B$550.
- *Costs the client:* paying ahead, and paying the transfer fees.

**4. Response Times**
- *Says:* a table for emergency, urgent and routine calls. 4.1 "respond" means a call back and "on site" means someone arrives to make safe. 4.2 after-hours call-outs are charged. 4.3 response times don't apply during storms, outages, no access or shipping delays. 4.4 for fire, gas, injury or crime, call 919 or 911 first, and BPL for downed lines.
- *Protects you:* a client abroad expects a plumber in 20 minutes at 11 pm on a Sunday. The table defines what "fast" means. 4.3 pauses your promise during a hurricane warning.
- *Costs the client:* they get defined response times, not instant ones. After-hours visits cost extra.

**5. Work Outside the Plan**
- *Says:* other work goes on a Work Order (Form A) at Schedule 2 rates or a fixed price. Approval is by signature, WhatsApp or email. Projects over B$[ ] get their own agreement.
- *Protects you:* same idea as change orders. Bigger projects get proper contract protection, not a one-page work order.
- *Costs the client:* approving work before it's done.

**6. Spending Approval**
- *Says:* 6.1 you may spend up to B$[ ] per item and B$[ ] per month without asking. 6.2 anything above needs written approval. 6.3 if you can't reach anyone within [2] hours in an emergency, you may spend up to the emergency limit. 6.4 everything spent is reported with receipts.
- *Protects you:* the tenant's AC isn't cooling and a capacitor plus an hour's work is inside the per-item limit, so you just fix it. A new compressor needs approval. Without limits, either nothing gets fixed without a three-day email chain, or you spend and the owner says "I never agreed".
- *Costs the client:* trusting you with small spends. They see every receipt.

**7. Keys, Access and Occupants**
- *Says:* the client provides keys and codes. You lock them away, tag them by code not address, log every use, and keep codes confidential. You're not responsible for others who hold keys. The client arranges tenant and guest notice. Short-term rental visits happen between bookings. Keys are returned at the end.
- *Protects you:* a unit is broken into and the owner asks who had keys. Your key log shows the last use, and 7.3 covers everyone else with a key. 7.4 keeps you out of a tenant's unit without proper notice.
- *Costs the client:* arranging tenant notice, and sharing the booking calendar.

**8. Reports and Records**
- *Says:* a report with Form B and dated photos within [2] working days of each visit; a monthly statement; recommendations are advice, and declined ones are noted; records kept [24] months.
- *Protects you:* in March you report a soft spot in the fascia and the owner declines the repair. In August a storm rips it off. 8.3 says that's not on you.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**9. Hurricane Preparation and Post-Storm Service**
- *Says:* the service is [included / an add-on / not taken]. Prep is done from the Schedule 1 list once a storm threatens. The client confirms at least [72] hours before landfall, and the crew stops about 48 hours before. After the storm you inspect within [48] hours once it's safe, report, and make safe. Add-on clients come first. Normal rates, no storm surcharge. You're not responsible for storm damage, except where you failed to do prep that was confirmed in time.
- *Protects you:* the cut-off and the crew release protect your people. "No storm surcharge" protects your name, because gouging after a storm is remembered for years.
- *Costs the client:* the add-on fee, and confirming in time.

**10. Pre-Existing Conditions and Items Not Maintained**
- *Says:* the baseline inspection records the starting condition. You're not responsible for conditions that were already there, items not ticked in Schedule 1, age and wear, damage by others, or declined work. Inspections are visual only, not a home inspection or termite certificate.
- *Protects you:* you take over a 30-year-old house, and in month 2 the drain under the slab, which had been rotting for years, fails. Baseline photos and 10.2(a) say it isn't yours.
- *Costs the client:* accepting those limits. They get a baseline report out of it.

**11. Warranty**
- *Says:* your own faulty workmanship on minor items and Work Orders is fixed free if reported within [90] days. Projects carry their own warranty.
- *Protects you:* small repairs get a short warranty to match their size.
- *Costs the client:* a shorter warranty than the 12 months on projects.

**12. Limit of Liability**
- *Says:* capped at the fees paid in the 12 months before the event. No loss of rent, booking cancellations or guest refunds. Gross negligence, wilful misconduct, fraud, and death or injury from negligence are not capped.
- *Protects you:* a guest cancels a B$2,400 week because the AC failed, and the owner claims it from you. 12.2 says no. Example cap: 12 months x B$550 = B$6,600 (whether Work Order charges count as "fees" is a question for your attorney).
- *Costs the client:* they can't recover more than a year's fees from you, or lost bookings, unless you were grossly negligent. Their insurance and booking platform cover the rest.

**13. Insurance**
- *Says:* your public liability; the client insures the properties and contents [and carries liability cover].
- *Protects you / costs the client:* same as small works clause 11.

**14. Late Payment and Suspension**
- *Says:* 14.1 interest. 14.2 if a sum is [7] days overdue, you may suspend all services, including emergencies, on [3] days' notice, and while suspended you're not responsible for what you'd have caught. 14.3 recovery costs. 14.4 title to materials.
- *Protects you:* without 14.2 you'd keep answering 2 am calls for a client three months behind.
- *Costs the client:* nothing if they pay. B$550 a month late costs B$8.25.

**15. Termination**
- *Says:* 15.1 end at the end of a term. 15.2 either side may end early on [60] days' notice. 15.3 either side may end for a breach not fixed in [14] days, and you may also end it on [7] days' notice if a sum is [30] days overdue. 15.4 a sold property can be removed on [30] days' notice. 15.5 settle up, return keys, and refund any unused prepaid fees.
- *Protects you:* a clean exit from a client who won't pay, with notice that protects your income.
- *Costs the client:* 60 days' notice to leave early.

**16. Disputes and Law**
- *Says:* meet (by video if abroad), [mediation], then courts or arbitration in The Bahamas. Undisputed sums can be claimed. Bahamian law, and a client abroad agrees disputes are decided in The Bahamas.
- *Protects you:* you don't want to defend a claim in Georgia. Your attorney confirms how well 16.4 holds up against a foreign client.
- *Costs the client:* coming here, or hiring a Bahamian attorney, if they want to fight.

**17. General**
- *Says:* notices (email is the main address for a client abroad; 5 pm Nassau time); the representative's approvals bind the client; confidentiality; subcontracting; entire agreement.
- *Protects you:* a notice sent to the listed email counts even if the client never opens it.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. Confidentiality protects them.

**Schedules and forms.** Schedule 1 lists the properties, the items maintained, the fee and the hurricane option. Schedule 2 is the rate card. Form A is the Work Order. Form B is the 14-line monthly inspection checklist.

## 5. Subcontract Agreement

Template: [subcontract-agreement.md](../templates/subcontract-agreement.md)

**What it is:** the contract between you and each sub. One per sub per job.
**Use it** every time, before the sub sets foot on site.

**Words:** [flow-down](01-glossary.md#f), [retention](01-glossary.md#r), [pay-when-paid](01-glossary.md#p), [backcharge](01-glossary.md#b), [direct dealing](01-glossary.md#d), [indemnity](01-glossary.md#i), [additional insured](01-glossary.md#a), [letter of good standing](01-glossary.md#l), [crew list](01-glossary.md#c), [variation](01-glossary.md#v), [work permit](01-glossary.md#w).

**First principle.** You carry the liability to the client for the sub's work and safety, even though you never touch a tool. The subcontract hands the same obligations down to the sub. It's also fair to the sub: they know exactly what they're doing, when they get paid and why money might be held. Good subs like clear paper.

### Clause by clause

**Top: parties and project**
- *Says:* the sub's legal name, licence, NIB employer number and VAT status; the project; the main contract.
- *Protects you:* a sub calls himself "Bethel Tile", but his licence says "Marvin Bethel trading as Bethel Tile Works". Use the licence name.
- *Costs the sub:* nothing.

**1. Scope**
- *Says:* 1.1 the exact work, tied to drawings. 1.2 what you supply. 1.3 what the sub supplies. 1.4 anything else needs a signed variation.
- *Protects you:* the tile man arrives and says "I don't supply thinset", and the job stops while you run to the hardware store. 1.2 and 1.3 settle who brings what before the day.
- *Costs the sub:* nothing. They know exactly what they priced.

**2. Flow-down**
- *Says:* the sub owes you, for their work, what you owe the client: quality, standards, warranty, schedule, site rules.
- *Protects you:* you give the Rolles 12 months' warranty. Without flow-down, the sub's cracked tile in month 9 is yours to fix at your cost.
- *Costs the sub:* the same standards you carry.

**3. Price**
- *Says:* 3.1 lump sum, unit rate or day rate, plus or minus VAT. 3.2 the price includes NIB, wages, overtime, transport and tools.
- *Protects you:* halfway through, the sub asks for B$60 extra "for transport". 3.2 says it's already in the price.
- *Costs the sub:* they price their own costs properly.

**4. Documents before starting, and 4.2 keeping them current**
- *Says:* no start until you have (a) the business licence, (b) an NIB letter of good standing, (c) public liability insurance naming you, (d) a crew list confirming everyone may legally work in The Bahamas, and (e) the trade licence where needed. 4.2: the sub keeps them current, and while any document is expired or missing you can hold payment and stop them working.
- *Protects you:* an uninsured sub's worker cuts his hand on a wet saw, there's no NIB standing behind him, and the claim lands on you. Under (d), an undocumented worker on your site can mean a raid, a fine and your name in the paper. Under 4.2, insurance that expires mid-job means work stops until it's renewed.
- *Costs the sub:* keeping papers current, which a real business does anyway.

**5. Payment**
- *Says:* 5.1 invoices for work done and accepted. 5.2 payment within [7] days after the client pays you for that work, with a [30]-day backstop if the client is holding back for your reasons or for reasons unrelated to the sub. 5.3 [10]% retention: half released at practical completion and owner acceptance, half at the end of the defects period. 5.4 nothing paid for defective work until it's fixed. 5.5 no advances unless agreed.
- *Protects you:* you pay the tile sub before the Rolles pay their tile stage. They then dispute the tile, and you're out of pocket. Retention: grout cracks in month 6, the sub doesn't answer, and you still hold his retention to pay someone else.
- *Costs the sub:* waiting for the client's payment (at most 30 days if the hold-up isn't his fault), and 10% held until the work is accepted.
- *Be fair:* when the client pays you for a sub's work, pay that sub within days. Your bench is your business. Your attorney settles the final wording of 5.2.

**6. Schedule**
- *Says:* start, duration and minimum crew. The sub follows your look-ahead, and catches up at their own cost if they fall behind through their own fault.
- *Protects you:* a sub who sends one man instead of two doubles your schedule. 6.1 fixes the minimum crew.
- *Costs the sub:* showing up as promised.

**7. Quality and Inspection**
- *Says:* 7.1 work to the drawings, code and manufacturer. 7.2 nothing covered up before inspection. 7.3 you can reject defective work in writing, and the sub fixes it at their own cost within [48] hours.
- *Protects you:* the sub tiles over the shower membrane before the flood test. If it leaks, all the tile comes off. 7.2 makes the flood test a hold point.
- *Costs the sub:* waiting for your check, and fixing their own defects.

**8. Variations**
- *Says:* no extra work is paid without your signed variation. Instructions from the client are not variations.
- *Protects you:* Mrs. Rolle asks the tile man for a shower niche. He builds it and bills you B$150. Clause 8 says it isn't paid unless you signed for it.
- *Costs the sub:* they send the client's requests to you.

**9. Site Rules and Safety**
- *Says:* PPE, fall protection above [6] feet, no alcohol or drugs, you can remove anyone unsafe, and every injury and near miss is reported the same day.
- *Protects you:* safety on your site is your responsibility. This lets you enforce it.
- *Costs the sub:* PPE and discipline.

**10. Clean-up**
- *Says:* daily clean-up. If the sub doesn't clean after [24] hours' notice, you clean and deduct the cost.
- *Protects you:* the client judges you by the mess.
- *Costs the sub:* half an hour a day.

**11. Backcharges**
- *Says:* written notice. If the failure isn't fixed in [48] hours (immediately for safety), you fix it and deduct the cost plus [10]%.
- *Protects you:* the painter leaves paint on the new tile and ignores your notice. You pay a cleaner B$200 and deduct B$220.
- *Costs the sub:* only if they ignore a notice.

**12. No Direct Dealing**
- *Says:* for [12] months the sub takes no instructions, payments or extra work from your client without your consent, and doesn't solicit them.
- *Protects you:* the tile man gives the Rolles his card and does their kitchen backsplash next month for cash. You lose the client, and the callbacks still come to you.
- *Costs the sub:* they can't take your client for 12 months.

**13. Warranty**
- *Says:* [12] months from practical completion of your main contract.
- *Protects you:* it matches the warranty you gave the client, so you can pass the fix to whoever caused it.
- *Costs the sub:* 12 months of callbacks on their own work.

**14. Indemnity and Insurance**
- *Says:* the sub covers claims caused by their work or people, except where you were negligent, and keeps their insurance in force.
- *Protects you:* the sub's worker drops a block on the neighbour's car. The claim goes to the sub's insurer, not to you.
- *Costs the sub:* insurance they should carry anyway.

**15. Termination**
- *Says:* 15.1 for default, after [3] days' written notice to fix (immediately for serious safety breaches or illegal workers). 15.2 you finish the work with others and deduct the extra cost. 15.3 if your main contract ends, the subcontract ends and the sub is paid for work done.
- *Protects you:* the block man disappears for two weeks. Letters 12A and 12B, then the next sub on your bench, and his extra cost comes out of what you owe the first one.
- *Costs the sub:* if they default, they pay the extra cost of replacing them.

**16. Disputes and Law**
- *Says:* talk within [7] days, then [mediation and arbitration / courts]. Bahamian law.
- *Protects you / costs the sub:* a set route.

### How to fill it in: worked example (tile sub, 2026-001-SC-02)

**The job.** On the Rolle bathroom (job 2026-001), the tile sub is subcontract 2026-001-SC-02, lump sum B$1,260, with 10% retention. The sub is not VAT registered. Example sub: Marvin Bethel trading as Bethel Tile Works. Write "2026-001-SC-02" at the top of the subcontract, and log it in the job-cost-tracker Commitments sheet the day you sign.

### The 3 mistakes people make with it

1. **Letting the sub start before the clause 4 documents are in your hand,** and not tracking expiry dates (4.2). Check office-tracker Subs & Documents before every start and every payment.
2. **A loose supply split.** If 1.2 and 1.3 don't say who brings the thinset, membrane and saw, the first morning is an argument.
3. **Paying ahead of accepted work, or messing up retention.** Forgetting to hold the 10%, or releasing all of it at completion before the defects period ends.

## 6. Change Order

Template: [change-order.md](../templates/change-order.md)

**What it is:** a one-page signed document that changes the scope, price or time of a contract.
**Use it** for every change, before the changed work starts. Small works clause 7, short-form term 6, subcontract clause 8 (where it's called a variation).

**Words:** [change order](01-glossary.md#c), [change order log](01-glossary.md#c), [variation](01-glossary.md#v), [markup](01-glossary.md#m), [extension of time](01-glossary.md#e).

### Section by section

**Header (project, site, contract date, owner, contractor, requested by, date)**
- *Says:* which contract this changes and whose idea it was.
- *Protects you:* "Requested by: Owner" stops the line "you suggested it, so it's free" at the end.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Description of change**
- *Says:* exactly what's added, removed or changed, with a sketch or photos.
- *Protects you:* "One more outlet" starts arguments. "One 20-amp GFCI duplex outlet on the wall opposite the vanity, 42 inches above the floor" doesn't.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Reason**
- *Says:* owner request, unforeseen condition, design change, or the inspector required it.
- *Protects you:* it shows the change wasn't your mistake.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Price table**
- *Says:* landed materials, labour or sub, equipment, subtotal, overhead and profit, any credit, total, VAT.
- *Protects you:* the client sees the math, not a number pulled from the air. Use the same markup as your estimate (small works 7.3).
- *Costs the client:* the price of the change.

**Effect on contract**
- *Says:* original price, previous change orders, this one, new price.
- *Protects you:* the final bill equals the contract plus signed change orders. No surprises, no arguments.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. They see the running total.

**Time**
- *Says:* days added, and the new completion date.
- *Protects you:* if you don't add the days here, the late finish is yours.
- *Costs the client:* a later finish, if the change takes time.

**Payment, and the signatures**
- *Says:* when the change is paid. Work starts after both sign.
- *Protects you:* no signature, no work. Big changes should be partly paid on signing.
- *Costs the client:* signing, and paying on the agreed terms.

### How to fill it in: worked example (CO-02, shower niche, Rolle job)

These are the same numbers as the spreadsheets and `07-a-job-start-to-finish.md`. Before tile starts, Mrs. Rolle asks for a recessed niche in the shower wall for shampoo bottles. CO-01 (rotted subfloor under the old tub, B$520, plus 1 working day) is already signed and paid.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Materials (landed): niche insert, membrane, trim (09-30-M) | 1 | lot | 65.00 | 65.00 |
| Labour / subcontract: tile sub variation on 2026-001-SC-02 (09-30-S) | 1 | ls | 150.00 | 150.00 |
| Equipment | | | | 0.00 |
| Subtotal | | | | 215.00 |
| Overhead and profit | | | | 60.00 |
| Credit for work removed | | | | 0.00 |
| **Total change** | | | | **275.00** |
| VAT (not registered) | | | | 0.00 |
| **Total including VAT** | | | | **275.00** |

Math: 215.00 / 0.78 = 275.64, rounded to B$275. Dividing by (1 - 0.22) keeps the same 22% margin as the estimate. Multiplying by 1.22 would not.

| Effect on contract | B$ |
|---|---|
| Original contract price | 11,375.00 |
| Previous approved change orders (CO-01) | 520.00 |
| This change order | 275.00 |
| **New contract price** | **12,170.00** |

Time: does not change the completion date (the tile sub builds it in while tiling). Payment: on signing, because it needs materials (SOP-09). Header: Requested by Owner (Rolle), [date]. Both sign, she pays, then you order the insert. Log it in the job-cost-tracker Change Orders sheet; it stays Pending until signed, then Approved.

### The 3 mistakes people make with it

1. **Doing the work first and pricing it later.** "We'll sort it out at the end" means you've lost: at the end you have no leverage.
2. **Writing "no change" to the time when the change does add days,** or leaving the change order out of the running total, so your invoices stop matching the contract.
3. **Pricing changes below your estimate markup to be nice,** or getting only your own signature on the form.

## 7. Proposal

Template: [quote-proposal.md](../templates/quote-proposal.md)

**What it is:** your written price offer: what you'll do, what's not included, the price, payment, time and warranty.
**Use it** for every job, before the contract. Once the client signs "Accepted", you've agreed on scope and price, but without the contract's protections (interest, suspension, hidden conditions, liability cap). That's why the contract follows straight after.

**Words:** [bid / quote / proposal](01-glossary.md#b), [validity date](01-glossary.md#v), [exclusions](01-glossary.md#e), [allowance](01-glossary.md#a).

### Section by section

**Header and "Valid for"**
- *Says:* your business details, the client, the proposal number, and how long the price holds.
- *Protects you:* if prices rise after 30 days, the offer has expired. Never make it longer than your supplier and sub quotes.
- *Costs the client:* they have to decide within the validity period.

**What we will do, Materials**
- *Says:* the work, line by line, plus brands, models and allowances.
- *Protects you:* this becomes Schedule A or page 1 of the contract, word for word.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Not included**
- *Says:* the exclusions. Always include: permit fees unless stated, hidden damage, utility connection fees, moving contents.
- *Protects you:* same as Schedule B.
- *Costs the client:* nothing, but they know what's extra.

**Price, Payment, Time, Warranty**
- *Says:* the summary terms.
- *Protects you:* the client agrees to the money shape before the contract arrives, so the contract holds no surprises.
- *Costs the client:* the price.

**Next step, and Accepted**
- *Says:* sign to accept; we then send the right contract (Short-Form up to about B$10,000, Small Works above) with the deposit invoice; the start date is booked when the contract is signed and the deposit clears.
- *Protects you:* the order is contract signed first, then deposit (SOP-03 steps 9 to 11; [contract-guide section 5](../templates/contract-guide.md#5-signing-rules)). Accepting a proposal is not a contract with all your protections in it. Never start on a signed proposal alone.

1. **A validity period longer than your supplier and sub quotes.** If their prices move, you're stuck with yours.
2. **An empty or thin "Not included".** Every missing exclusion is a future argument.
3. **Taking a deposit on the proposal and never signing the contract,** or emailing a PDF without walking the client through it (SOP-03 step 3).

## 8. Payment Application

Template: [payment-application.md](../templates/payment-application.md)

**What it is:** your invoice. It shows the whole contract, so the client sees what's been earned, what's been paid and what's left.
**Use it** at every payment stage, including the deposit.

**Words:** [payment application](01-glossary.md#p), [milestone billing](01-glossary.md#m), [retention](01-glossary.md#r), [WIP](01-glossary.md#w).

### Section by section

**Header**
- *Says:* invoice number, your details, VAT TIN, the client, project, date, period.
- *Protects you:* numbered invoices (2026-001-INV-01, -02...) can't be lost or confused. If you're not VAT registered, write "not VAT registered" where the template says VAT TIN.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

**Progress by stage**
- *Says:* for each stage: contract value, % complete, earned to date, previously billed, this invoice. Approved change orders get their own line.
- *Protects you:* "I already paid that" is answered by the "previously billed" column.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. They see exactly what they're paying for.

**Summary**
- *Says:* earned to date, less any retention the client holds, less previously paid, the amount due, VAT, total.
- *Protects you:* the math is on the page, so there's nothing to argue about.
- *Costs the client:* the amount due.

**Due date, Pay to, Attached**
- *Says:* the due date and the clause it comes from, interest if late, your bank details, and the attached photos and change orders.
- *Protects you:* dated photos prove the stage is done. The template lists the payment clause for each agreement (Small Works clause 4, Short-Form page 1 payment table, T&M clause 7.2, Maintenance clause 3.2). Delete the ones that don't apply.
- *Costs the client:* nothing.

### How to fill it in: worked example (Rolle, 2026-001-INV-02, stage 2)

### The 3 mistakes people make with it

1. **Billing a stage before it's done, or billing late.** Early billing burns trust. Late billing means you're funding the job. Invoice the day the stage is reached (Friday rhythm, [05-spreadsheets-explained.md](05-spreadsheets-explained.md)).
2. **Including change orders that aren't signed yet, or leaving out signed ones.**
3. **Charging VAT when you aren't registered, or math that doesn't tie.** Earned minus previously paid must equal this invoice.

## 9. Notice Letters

Template: [notice-letters.md](../templates/notice-letters.md)

**What they are:** twelve ready-to-send letters. Each one puts a fact on record and uses a contract clause at the right moment. Letters 1 to 9 go to clients, using small works clause numbers. Letters 10 to 12 go to subs, using subcontract clause numbers. The template's clause table converts the numbers for the short form, T&M and maintenance agreements.

**Words:** [notice](01-glossary.md#n), [extension of time](01-glossary.md#e), [right to suspend](01-glossary.md#r), [demand letter](01-glossary.md#d), [backcharge](01-glossary.md#b), [termination](01-glossary.md#t).

**First principle.** Many of your rights exist only if you give written notice on time. A right you didn't use in writing is a right you don't have. And memory loses arguments; letters win them.

### Letter by letter

| # | Letter | Send when | Uses (small works / subcontract) | Deadline | What it gets you | What it costs the other side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirmation of verbal instruction | Someone asks for something not in scope, or gives a decision | 7, 7.1 | Same day | Turns a conversation into a record; no free extras | Confirming or correcting in writing |
| 2 | Unforeseen condition | You open up and find something you couldn't see | 8, 7.2, 5.3(e) | Same day, before fixing | Payment and time for hidden work | A decision on the fix |
| 3 | Delay and extension of time | A delay you didn't cause | 5.3 | Within 7 days | The finish date moves | A later finish |
| 4 | Hurricane (A before, B after) | A storm threatens; after you inspect | 5.3(a), 11.2, 12.3 | About 5 days out; after the storm | Time, a reminder about insurance, the site secured | Securing their own things |
| 5 | Friendly payment reminder | 1 to 3 days after the due date | 4.1, 4.2 | Days 1 to 3 | Payment without a fight | Nothing |
| 6 | Notice of intent to suspend | 7 days overdue | 4.3 | Day 7 | Starts the 3 days' notice | Pay, or the job stops |
| 7 | Suspension notice | After the date in letter 6 | 4.3, 4.4 (optional 15.1) | The day after | You stop legally; remobilisation costs are recoverable | A stopped job, plus costs |
| 8 | Final demand | About 14 days after suspension or completion | 4.2, 4.5, 16 | Before the attorney | Last chance before legal costs | Legal costs if they ignore it |
| 9 | Practical completion and punch list walk | Done except minor items | 14, 4.1, 12.1 | When done | Starts the completion payment and the defects period | A walk within 3 days |
| 10 | Defective work (to sub) | The sub's work fails inspection | SC 7.3, 7.2, 5.4, 11 | At inspection | Fixed at the sub's cost; no pay until fixed | Fixing it within 48 hours |
| 11 | Backcharge (A notice, B statement) | The sub fails to do something | SC 10, 11 | A: at the failure; B: after you fix it | You deduct the cost (plus 10% under 11) | Paying for what they skipped |
| 12 | Sub default (A notice, B termination) | The sub stops or keeps failing | SC 15.1, 15.2 | A: 3 days to fix; B: after that (immediate for serious safety or illegal workers) | You replace the sub and deduct the extra cost | Losing the job, and the extra cost |

**The late-payment ladder (small works numbers).** Say the due date is day 0.

| Day | Letter | Why that day |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | 5: friendly reminder | Most late payers just forgot |
| 7 | 6: intent to suspend | Clause 4.3: 7 days overdue, then 3 days' notice |
| 10 | 7: suspension | The notice period has run |
| About 24 | 8: final demand | Last step before the attorney |

### How to fill it in: worked example (letter 6, Rolle job)

Invoice 2026-001-INV-03 for stage 3 (B$2,843.75) was due on [date]. Seven days later it's unpaid, and the reminder (letter 5) went out on day 2.

- **To / Date / Re:** Kendra and Marcus Rolle / [day 7 date] / Notice under clause 4.3. Invoice 2026-001-INV-03, bathroom renovation, Lot 27, [street], off Carmichael Road.
- **Overdue:** "Invoice 2026-001-INV-03 for B$2,843.75 was due on [date] and is now 7 days overdue. We sent a reminder on [date]."
- **Interest to date:** one month's interest is 2,843.75 x 1.5% = B$42.66. Seven days is 42.66 x 7 / 30 = B$9.95. Your attorney confirms how part-months should be counted.
- **Deadline:** at least 3 days after the notice counts as received. If you send it by 5 pm on a business day, it counts that day (clause 17.3).
- **Send:** WhatsApp and email to the addresses in clause 17.3, plus a signed printed copy delivered by hand. Screenshot the WhatsApp delivery ticks. File a copy.
- **Then:** call them the same day. Every letter is firmer when you've also picked up the phone.

### The 3 mistakes people make with it

1. **Sending late.** A delay notice after the 7 days, or an unforeseen condition notice after you've already fixed the problem.
2. **Skipping steps.** Suspending without letter 6's notice period, or terminating a sub without letter 12A. Skip the step and the breach becomes yours.
3. **Angry tone, empty threats, or no proof of sending.** Write as if a judge will read it, because one day one might.

## 10. Receipts and Releases

Template: [receipts-and-releases.md](../templates/receipts-and-releases.md)

**What it is:** five forms. Form 1 is a receipt for every client payment. Forms 2 and 3 are receipts with releases for every sub payment. Form 4 is the completion certificate. Form 5 is the warranty certificate.

**Words:** [retention](01-glossary.md#r), [practical completion](01-glossary.md#p), [defects liability period](01-glossary.md#d), [punch list](01-glossary.md#p), [warranty](01-glossary.md#w), [closeout](01-glossary.md#c).

**First principle.** A *release* means the person signing gives up claims up to a point. That's why you read a release to a sub before they sign it, and why your attorney checks Forms 2 and 3 once.

### Form by form

**Form 1. Client payment receipt**
- *Says:* what was received, from whom, for what, how it was paid, and the balance left. A cheque counts only when it clears.
- *Protects you:* a running record of every payment. With cash in particular, "I paid you" gets answered by your numbered receipt book. Cash is legal; unreceipted cash is how VAT and licence tax get dodged, and you don't do that.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. It protects them more than you.

**Form 2. Sub payment receipt and partial release**
- *Says:* the sub confirms they received the payment and were paid in full up to a date, except retention and any claims they list. They've paid their workers, NIB and suppliers for that period.
- *Protects you:* six months after the job, the sub claims B$300 for "extra prep". Form 2, signed at each payment, says there were no claims up to that date except those listed.
- *Costs the sub:* they must raise claims when they're paid, not save them up.

**Form 3. Sub final payment and release of claims**
- *Says:* the final account. The sub confirms full and final settlement except any retention balance (stated, with its date), releases other claims, and confirms the warranty and other duties continue.
- *Protects you:* the job is closed with that sub. No surprise claims later.
- *Costs the sub:* giving up claims they didn't list. The retention balance is protected because it's written in.

**Form 4. Certificate of practical completion and client acceptance**
- *Says:* the walk date, the punch list with fix dates, what was handed over, the defects period dates, the completion payment and holdback. From that date the client is responsible for insurance, security, use and maintenance. Signing doesn't take away their warranty.
- *Protects you:* it fixes the date the warranty clock starts, and moves risk for the finished work to the owner.
- *Costs the client:* taking over the finished work. The last line, about keeping the warranty, is what gets it signed.

**Form 5. Warranty certificate**
- *Says:* 12 months on workmanship, manufacturers' warranties listed, exclusions, how to make a claim (in writing with photos, response times, give access, don't let others repair it), and whether it transfers.
- *Protects you:* claims come in properly, with photos, within the period, and the exclusions are written where the owner will read them.
- *Costs the client:* nothing. It's their proof of warranty.

### How to fill it in: worked examples (Rolle job)

**Form 1, deposit receipt.** Receipt no. 0001 (one running series for the business). Received from Kendra and Marcus Rolle. Project: bathroom renovation, Lot 27, off Carmichael Road. Agreement dated [ ]. For: deposit. Amount B$4,550.00; VAT not charged; total B$4,550.00. Paid by bank transfer, ref [ ]. **Form 2, tile sub, stage 1.**

|---|---|
| Subcontract sum including variations | 1,260.00 |
| Value of work done and accepted to [date] | 630.00 |
| Less retention 10% | (63.00) |

Line 2(a): retention held B$63.00. Claims not released: "None". Signed by Marvin Bethel, witnessed.

**Form 3, tile sub, at practical completion.** Original sum 1,260.00; variations 0.00; backcharges 0.00; final subcontract sum 1,260.00; paid to date 1,134.00; retention released in this payment 63.00; **final payment now 63.00**; retention still held **63.00, due [12 months after practical completion]**. When you pay that last B$63 a year later, the sub signs a Form 2 marked "retention balance, final", with no claims listed.

**Form 4, Rolle handover.** Walked on [date]. Practical completion [date]. Punch list: 1. re-caulk the shower door sill; 2. touch up paint by the door frame; 3. adjust the vanity door hinge. ### The 3 mistakes people make with it

1. **Paying a sub before they sign Form 2** (or Form 3 for the last payment). Once the money's gone, the signature is hard to get.
2. **A release that doesn't state the retention still owed.** Then the sub thinks they signed it away, or you're tempted to treat it that way. Always fill in "retention still held" and its date.
3. **No receipt for cash, or keys handed over without a signed Form 4.** With no practical completion date, nobody knows when the warranty starts or ends.

## How to explain a contract to a client in 2 minutes

Use the script in [contract-guide.md, section 4](../templates/contract-guide.md#4-how-to-present-it-so-it-gets-signed), and the "When they push back" answers right below it. Don't read the clauses aloud. Walk through five things, pointing at the page: what you're doing, what's not included, how payment works, how changes work, the warranty. Say it the way the script says it: "this protects both of us".

When the client asks "what does that mean for me?", use the **Costs the client** line for that clause in this guide. Say the cost plainly, then the reason. Clients trust the contractor who tells them the cost before they find it.
