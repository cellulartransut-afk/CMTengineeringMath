# 07. A job from start to finish

Rev 1 · 2026-11-04

This guide follows one bathroom job from the first phone call to the last retention payment 12 months later. It uses every SOP, form and spreadsheet in the order you would use them on a real job.

The numbers match the example already loaded in the spreadsheets (job 2026-001), so you can open the files and follow along. The spreadsheet example stops in the middle of the job. From there the story carries on to the end with the same method.

The client's name and the days are made up. A word in **bold** the first time is explained right there, or look it up in [01-glossary.md](01-glossary.md).

## 1. The phone call

**What happens.** Mrs. Rolle calls on a Wednesday afternoon. Your aunt gave her your number. Her tub leaks at one corner and she wants a walk-in shower. She is a **lead**: anyone who might hire you. On this call you are not selling. You are finding out whether she can and will pay, and booking a visit.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-01](../operations-manual/sop-01-leads-and-client-vetting.md) | [checklists.md](../templates/checklists.md) section 1, Vet a new client (explained in [02-checklists-explained.md](02-checklists-explained.md)) | [office-tracker.xlsx](../tools/office-tracker.xlsx), Leads & Quotes |

**Do it:**
1. Answer, or call back within 2 working hours.
2. Ask the **vetting** questions (vetting means checking a client before you spend time on them). Her answers: she owns the house and pays for it herself. Her budget is "about B$12,000". She wants it done within two months. No other contractor has started. There are no drawings.
3. Tell her your terms on the same call (script below). She says that's fine.
4. Check fit. A bathroom suits your stage and there are no red flags, so it's a go.
5. Book the site visit within 3 working days: Friday at 4 pm.
6. Give the job the next job number, 2026-001. You give the number when the site visit is booked, not when the contract is signed. Lost quotes keep their number too.
7. Type the row in Leads & Quotes:

| Date | Name | Source | Area | Job type | Site visit | Job no. | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| YYYY-MM-DD | Mrs. Rolle | Referral | Carmichael | Bathroom renovation | Friday 4 pm | 2026-001 | Site visit booked |

**Say it:**
> "Before I come, let me tell you how I work so there are no surprises. Everything goes in a written contract. I take a 40% deposit before I order anything, then you pay in stages as the work gets done. Is that OK with you?"

> WhatsApp after the call: "Good afternoon Mrs. Rolle, this is [name] from [business]. Confirming Friday at 4 pm at your home in Carmichael. I'll need about 45 minutes to measure and take photos. Your job reference is 2026-001. Thank you."

**File it:** Create the folder `Jobs/2026-001 Rolle Bathroom-Reno Carmichael/` with subfolders 01 Contract to 09 Closeout ([SOP-15](../operations-manual/sop-15-document-control-and-filing.md)). Your call notes go in 02 Estimate.

**What can go wrong:** You don't answer and she calls the next contractor. You skip the terms, then find out at proposal time that she won't pay a deposit. Walk away from anyone who refuses a deposit, wants to skip the permit, or wants to pay cash with no invoice.

**Why:** An estimate costs you days of work. Spend those days only on clients who can pay.

## 2. The site visit

**What happens.** You go, look, measure and photograph. This is where most missing items get caught, or get missed.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-02](../operations-manual/sop-02-site-visit-and-estimating.md) steps 1 to 6 | A paper sketch | None yet |

**Do it:**
1. The day before: read your notes, write your questions, charge your phone, and pack a tape, laser measure, level, torch and notepad.
2. Arrive on time. Let her walk you through it and talk first.
3. Measure the floor, the walls to the ceiling, the tub area, the door and the window. Write every dimension on a sketch while you're there.
4. Photograph every wall, the floor, the ceiling and the water stain at the tub corner. Also photograph the water heater, the electrical panel, the hallway and front steps (the old tub leaves that way), and where the **skip bin** will sit. A skip bin is the large steel debris container a truck drops off and collects.
5. Write down what you can't see: under the tub, inside the walls, the pipes. That tub corner has leaked for years. Expect damage under it.
6. Within 24 hours, send your questions in writing.

**Say it:**
> "Thank you for your time today Mrs. Rolle. Three questions so I price it right: 1. Walk-in shower in place of the tub, correct? 2. Do you want a shower niche (the shelf built into the wall)? 3. Is a standard 24 x 24 porcelain floor tile OK, with you choosing the colour? Your proposal will be ready by Thursday."

Her reply: yes to the shower, yes to the standard tile. On the niche: "Maybe later. It's a budget thing."

**File it:** The sketch goes in 02 Estimate. Photos go in `08 Photos/YYYY-MM-DD/`.

**What can go wrong:** You miss a measurement and guess it later. You don't photograph the stain, and later it becomes "your men caused that".

**Why:** You can only price what you saw and wrote down.

## 3. The estimate

**What happens.** You work out what the job will cost **you**. The price comes after. An **estimate** is a prediction of cost. If the cost is wrong, no markup saves you.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-02](../operations-manual/sop-02-site-visit-and-estimating.md) steps 7 to 18; [SOP-04](../operations-manual/sop-04-subcontractor-selection-and-buyout.md) steps 1 to 5 (sub bids) | [checklists.md](../templates/checklists.md) section 3, Before you send a quote | [estimator.xlsx](../tools/estimator.xlsx): Assumptions, Landed Cost, Job Estimate, Markup Margin, Cash Flow. [cost-code-book.xlsx](../tools/cost-code-book.xlsx): Cost Codes, Unit Cost Book. [job-cost-tracker.xlsx](../tools/job-cost-tracker.xlsx): Sub Bids |

**Do it:**
1. Pick the contract type: **lump sum**, meaning one fixed price for a written scope. You can see the whole scope except the floor under the tub. Don't guess at that. Write it out of the price as an exclusion (stage 4), so if it's rotten it becomes a paid change.
2. Make the job's tracker copy now: `2026-001 job-cost-tracker.xlsx`, saved in the job folder root. You need its Sub Bids sheet.
3. List the work items in build order and give each a **cost code**, the short number that files every cost in the right bucket. Demolition is 02-20. Tile by a sub is 09-30-S. Plumbing comes from division 15. Look the codes up in Cost Codes.
4. Take off quantities in Job Estimate and show the math. For example: tile = floor area + shower wall area, plus 10% to 15% waste.
5. Price materials **landed**, meaning the full cost delivered to site, including freight, duty, broker and trucking. The Landed Cost sheet shows the 24 x 24 tile lands at about B$3.88 a sq ft from Florida against B$5.25 locally, so you import it. Thinset lands at B$33.24 a bag against B$32 locally, so you buy that locally.
6. Get 3 written prices for each sub trade. Then **level the bids**: put them side by side on the same scope, because subs leave things out to look cheap. You asked the tile bidders to price the niche too, in case she wants it later:

| Tile bids (Sub Bids sheet) | Bidder 1 | Bidder 2 | Bidder 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base bid | B$1,260 | B$1,050 | B$1,450 |
| Left out | The niche (+B$150) | B$570 of the listed scope | Nothing |
| **Adjusted bid** | **B$1,410** | **B$1,620** | **B$1,450** |
| Documents | OK | None | OK |
| Result | Real low bid | Don't use | Second choice |

   The cheapest number, Bidder 2, is actually the most expensive job, and has no licence, NIB letter or insurance on file.

7. Add **general conditions**: the costs of running the job that belong to no single trade. Here that means the skip bin and dump fees, floor protection from the front door to the bathroom, final clean, and your travel.
8. Add **contingency**, money for problems that are yours to absorb: 5%, so B$8,450 x 5% = B$422.50. Renovation normally carries 10% to 15%. Here 5% is enough because the biggest unknown, the floor under the tub, is excluded and would be a paid change. If you can't write the unknown out of the price, carry 10% to 15%.
9. Set the price on **margin**, the share of the price you keep, not on **markup**, the percentage you add to cost:

| Line | B$ |
|---|---|
| Direct cost | 8,450.00 |
| Contingency 5% | 422.50 |
| **Total cost** | **8,872.50** |
| Price = cost / (1 - 0.22) = 8,872.50 / 0.78 | **11,375.00** |
| Gross profit = 11,375 - 8,872.50 | 2,502.50 |
| Gross margin = 2,502.50 / 11,375 | 22.0% |
| Same thing as markup = 2,502.50 / 8,872.50 | 28.2% |

   The trap: cost x 1.22 = B$10,824.45. That's a margin of only 18.0%, and B$550.55 of your profit is gone without you noticing.

10. Price the likely change in advance. If she adds the niche, it costs you B$150 (tile sub) + B$65 (niche kit and membrane) = B$215. Price = 215 / 0.78 = B$275.64, rounded to **B$275**.
11. Run the Cash Flow sheet with 40% / 25% / 25% / 10% stages. The example shows a positive cash position at every stage. If any stage showed NEGATIVE, you would change the stages before sending.
12. Check price per sq ft against the Unit Cost Book. On your first job there's nothing to compare against, so ask a contractor you trust whether B$11,375 is about right for a full bathroom in Carmichael.
13. Run checklist section 3, every line.

**File it:** In 02 Estimate: the estimate, supplier quotes, sub quotes and the leveling sheet.

**What can go wrong:** You miss an item, price shelf cost instead of landed cost, confuse markup with margin, or pick Bidder 2.

**Why:** The job is won or lost in the estimate, not on site.

## 4. The proposal

**What happens.** The **proposal** is your price on paper, with exactly what is in and what is out.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-03](../operations-manual/sop-03-proposal-contract-and-deposit.md) steps 1 to 5 | [quote-proposal.md](../templates/quote-proposal.md) | office-tracker Leads & Quotes (status Quoted, follow-ups) |

**Do it:**
1. Fill in the proposal. Write the scope line by line. Under materials: 24 x 24 porcelain, colour chosen by her from the supplier's range.
2. Under **Not included** (the **exclusions**), write in plain words: "Repair of rotten or damaged floor or framing found after the tub is removed. Priced as a change if found." Add permit fees, utility fees and moving contents.
3. Add the option: "Shower niche: B$275, if added before the shower walls are waterproofed."
4. Price B$11,375. VAT: not applicable, because you're not registered.
5. Payment stages:

| Stage | % | B$ |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit on signing | 40% | 4,550.00 |
| Demolition and rough-in complete | 25% | 2,843.75 |
| Finishes installed | 25% | 2,843.75 |
| Completion | 10% | 1,137.50 |
| **Total** | 100% | **11,375.00** |

6. Valid for 30 days, which is no longer than your supplier and sub quotes are valid.
7. Deliver it in person and walk her through the scope, the exclusions and the payments.
8. Follow up on day 3, day 7, and 3 days before it expires. Log each follow-up in Leads & Quotes. Status: Quoted.

She asks: "Can you do it for B$10,500?"

**Say it:**
> "I can't take B$875 off the same job and still do it right. What I can do is take scope out. If you keep your existing vanity and pick the basic fixture package, I can get close to B$10,500. Or we keep everything at B$11,375. Your choice."

She keeps the full scope and accepts.

**File it:** The proposal goes in 01 Contract as `YYYY-MM-DD proposal Rolle bathroom`.

**What can go wrong:** You send a PDF with no explanation and never follow up. Or you drop the price without dropping scope, and give away your profit.

**Why:** Scope you didn't write down is scope you do for free.

## 5. Contract and deposit

**What happens.** You turn the accepted proposal into a signed contract, then collect a **deposit** that has cleared before you spend a dollar.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-03](../operations-manual/sop-03-proposal-contract-and-deposit.md) steps 6 to 13 | [contract-guide.md](../templates/contract-guide.md) points to [owner-contract-small-works.md](../templates/owner-contract-small-works.md). Deposit invoice on [payment-application.md](../templates/payment-application.md). Receipt from [receipts-and-releases.md](../templates/receipts-and-releases.md). Clauses explained in [06-contracts-explained.md](06-contracts-explained.md) | job-cost-tracker Billing. office-tracker Receivables, Leads & Quotes (Won) |

**Do it:**
1. Fill every [ ] in the small works contract. Use her full legal name as the property owner. Copy Schedule A (scope) and Schedule B (exclusions) word for word from the proposal, including the floor-under-the-tub exclusion.
2. Check these clauses are set: payment table (clause 4.1); late interest 1.5% a month (4.2); right to suspend after 7 days late, on 3 days' notice (4.3); materials stay yours until paid (4.4); start date is the later of deposit cleared, permit issued and access given (5.1); 15 working days; she pays permit fees (6(c)); changes only by signed change order (7); hidden damage is extra (8); 12-month warranty (12).
3. Go through payments, changes, hidden damage and warranty with her in plain words before she signs.
4. Both of you sign, with a witness. She gets her copy the same day.
5. She picks the tile colour at signing. The tile is imported and needs 2 to 4 weeks, so it can't be ordered until she chooses.
6. Issue the deposit invoice, **2026-001-INV-01**, for B$4,550, the same day.
7. Wait until the money has **cleared**, meaning it's in your business account and spendable. "I sent it" isn't cleared. A cheque isn't cleared until the bank says so.
8. Send a receipt within 24 hours.
9. Tax money: you're not VAT registered and have no employees, so there's nothing to move to the tax account yet ([SOP-14](../operations-manual/sop-14-bookkeeping-monthly-close-and-taxes.md)).
10. Record INV-01 in Billing and Receivables. Mark the lead Won in Leads & Quotes.

**Say it:**
> "Thank you Mrs. Rolle. Your signed copy is attached, with invoice 2026-001-INV-01 for the B$4,550 deposit. Bank details are on the invoice. As soon as it clears, I order your tile and fixtures and book the plumber and tile setter."

> "Deposit received and cleared. Receipt attached. Tile is ordered today."

**File it:** In 01 Contract: `YYYY-MM-DD contract Rolle bathroom`. In 06 Invoices and Payments: `YYYY-MM-DD invoice 2026-001-INV-01 deposit`, the bank confirmation, and the receipt.

**What can go wrong:** You order tile on "I sent it" and the money never comes. Or the contract scope differs from the proposal, and the client holds you to the wider one.

**Why:** The Bahamas has no **lien** law, so you can't make a claim against the house if she doesn't pay. The contract and the deposit are your only security.

## 6. Buyout: hiring the subs

**What happens.** **Buyout** means signing every sub and every purchase order right after the deposit clears. That locks in your real costs.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-04](../operations-manual/sop-04-subcontractor-selection-and-buyout.md) steps 6 to 13 | [subcontract-agreement.md](../templates/subcontract-agreement.md). [checklists.md](../templates/checklists.md) section 2 | job-cost-tracker: Commitments, Budget & Cost Report. office-tracker: Subs & Documents. [subs.md](../my-business/subs.md) |

**Do it:**
1. Within 5 working days of the deposit clearing, confirm scope, price and dates with the plumber (B$1,650) and with tile Bidder 1 (B$1,260, without the niche, because the contract has no niche).
2. Collect documents before anyone signs: business licence, NIB letter of good standing, a public liability insurance certificate naming you, a crew list confirming everyone is legal to work in The Bahamas, and the plumber's trade licence. Type every expiry date into Subs & Documents.
3. Subs & Documents flags the tile sub's insurance as "Expiring within 30 days". Ask for the renewal now. Subcontract clause 4.2 says he must keep documents current, and you may hold payment while one has expired.
4. Fill in the subcontracts:
   - **2026-001-SC-01, plumber, B$1,650.** 50% when the rough-in passes inspection. 50% when the fixtures are set, tested and pass final.
   - **2026-001-SC-02, tile, B$1,260.** 100% on completion and acceptance. The niche is not included; if added, it comes by written variation at B$150.
   - Both: 10% **retention**. That's money you hold back until the work is accepted and the warranty period is clean. Half is released at practical completion, the other half after 12 months.
5. Sign before they start. No signature, no start.
6. Enter both in Commitments with their codes and 10% retention. A **commitment** is money you've agreed to pay, even before you pay it. Check the plumbing and tile lines in Budget & Cost Report to see whether buyout came in under or over budget.
7. Tell the other bidders.
8. Hold a pre-start meeting with each sub before their first day: scope, schedule, site rules, how they get paid.
9. Any other sub on the job, such as the carpenter who does demolition and the floor, goes through the same steps and gets the next SC number.

**Say it:**
> To Bidder 1: "You have the tile job on 2026-001, B$1,260 per your quote. Subcontract attached; please sign and send it back with your renewed insurance certificate. The start is around day 9. I'll confirm with the look-ahead."

> To Bidder 2: "Thanks for pricing 2026-001. We went with another bid this time. To be considered next time, I'll need your business licence, NIB letter and insurance certificate on file."

**File it:** Subcontracts and sub documents go in 04 Subs and POs (`YYYY-MM-DD subcontract 2026-001-SC-01 plumber`). Master copies of each sub's documents go in `Company/Legal and Licences/Subcontractor Documents/`.

**What can go wrong:** A sub starts "and we'll sign later". A sub with no insurance gets hurt on your job. You pay an advance and the sub disappears.

**Why:** Subcontracting the work doesn't subcontract the liability. The paper is how you control the people you're responsible for.

## 7. Buying materials

**What happens.** You order what the job needs, long-lead items first, and make sure what arrives is what you paid for.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-05](../operations-manual/sop-05-purchasing-and-materials.md) | A written purchase order (PO). Deliveries section of [daily-report.md](../templates/daily-report.md) | estimator Landed Cost. job-cost-tracker: Commitments, Actual Costs, Look-Ahead |

**Do it:**
1. Within 2 working days of the deposit clearing, build the buy list from the estimate, by cost code.
2. Order the **long-lead** item first. That's anything that takes weeks to arrive. Here it's the imported tile, 2 to 4 weeks from Florida, ordered through a licensed customs broker on its own PO. Ask Mrs. Rolle whether she has any duty concession. She doesn't. Put the lead time on the Look-Ahead.
3. Write **2026-001-PO-01**, B$1,350, to the local supplier for the fixtures (toilet, vanity, shower valve and trim, taps). List items, quantities, prices, delivery date and site address. Log it in Commitments under its division 15 code.
4. The day before delivery, confirm stock and the delivery time in writing.
5. Check the delivery against the **delivery note** (the paper listing what was sent) before you sign. One toilet carton arrives crushed. Write "1 toilet carton crushed, not accepted" on the note, photograph it, and have the supplier replace it.
6. Store everything in her garage, locked and off the floor. Valves and copper don't stay unsecured on site overnight.
7. When the invoice comes, match it to the PO and the signed delivery note. Then enter it in Actual Costs with the code, and write the code on the paper invoice too.
8. Pay on the supplier's terms. A new account is usually cash on delivery; once you've built credit, it becomes 30 days.

**Say it:**
> "Good morning. PO 2026-001-PO-01 attached for job 2026-001: fixtures as listed, B$1,350. Delivery to [address], Carmichael, Monday by 10 am please. Call me 30 minutes before. Thank you."

**File it:** POs and broker papers go in 04 Subs and POs. Supplier invoices go in 06. Delivery checks go in the daily report, and photos of the damage in 08.

**What can go wrong:** You order the tile late and the job sits waiting. You sign a delivery note clean and then find the cracked toilet, and the supplier owes you nothing.

**Why:** Materials are a big share of your cost. Every one lost, broken or late comes straight out of profit.

## 8. Getting ready to start

**What happens.** You get everything in place before anyone picks up a tool.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-06](../operations-manual/sop-06-job-start.md); [SOP-12](../operations-manual/sop-12-safety-incidents-and-hurricanes.md) for the hurricane plan | Preconstruction checklist in [05-field-playbook.md](../knowledge/05-field-playbook.md) | job-cost-tracker: Job Info, Budget & Cost Report, Look-Ahead |

**Do it:**
1. Fill in Job Info: 2026-001, client, site, lump sum, original price B$11,375, client holds no retention, VAT registered No. Load the budget from the estimate, line by line, by cost code.
2. Ask Building Control in writing whether the job needs a permit. **In this story** the answer is yes, because the plumbing moves, with a plumbing rough-in inspection and a final. Your job may be different, so always ask and get the answer in writing. Apply with a complete package and answer any query the same day. She pays the fees (clause 6(c)). The start date waits for the permit (clause 5.1), so a slow permit doesn't make you late.
3. Insurance: check your public liability certificate is current. Ask her to confirm in writing that her home insurance covers the house during the works (clause 11.2).
4. Hurricane plan: required if any work falls between June 1 and November 30. This job starts in December, so you don't need one. Had it started a week earlier, you'd write one page: skip bin emptied, materials in the garage, everything outside tied down or taken away.
5. Photograph and video the existing conditions: the bathroom, the hallway floor, door frames, front steps and driveway. Send her a copy.
6. Build the **look-ahead**, the day-by-day plan for the next three weeks, and send it to her and the subs:

| 5 to 8 | Floor and walls prepared, shower base | Carpenter, tile sub | |
| 9 | Waterproofing membrane | Tile sub SC-02 | |

| 15 | Final inspection, clean, punch walk | You, Building Control | |

7. December jobs run into Christmas, Boxing Day and the Junkanoo season. Ask every sub which days they're off and put those on the Look-Ahead now.
8. Hold the **kickoff meeting** with her (the first meeting before work starts). Cover: you're each other's one contact, how changes work, payment dates, and a weekly update every Friday by WhatsApp. Send written notes within 24 hours.
9. Give the neighbours a note: start date, work hours 7:30 am to 5 pm, and your number.
10. Set up the site: floor protection from the front door to the bathroom, first aid kit, emergency numbers (919 or 911) posted, permit posted, skip bin on her driveway.

**Say it:**
> Kickoff notes: "Thank you for meeting today. Agreed: start Monday; working days Monday to Friday, 7:30 to 5; you and I are the only two who agree changes, in writing and signed; next payment is due when demolition and rough-in are done (about day 4); I'll send photos and an update every Friday. Schedule attached."

**File it:** Permit, inspection list and site plan go in 03 Permits and Drawings. Schedule and kickoff notes go in 01 Contract. Existing-condition photos go in 08 Photos.

**What can go wrong:** You start before the permit and get stopped. You have no "before" photos, and she says your men scratched the hallway floor.

**Why:** A problem you prevent before day 1 costs nothing. The same problem on site costs days.

## 9. Every day on site

**What happens.** You run the site in this order every day: safety, quality, schedule, cost. Then you write it down.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |
|---|---|---|
| [SOP-07](../operations-manual/sop-07-daily-site-management.md); [SOP-12](../operations-manual/sop-12-safety-incidents-and-hurricanes.md) | [daily-report.md](../templates/daily-report.md) | job-cost-tracker: Look-Ahead, Actual Costs |

**Do it every day:**
1. Get there before the crew and walk the site for hazards.
2. Give a 5-minute **toolbox talk**, a short safety talk on one topic. For demolition: water shut off, bathroom circuits switched off by the electrician, dust masks, eye protection, gloves and boots. The old cast-iron tub is very heavy, so break it up or carry it with enough people.
3. If old floor tiles or pipe wrap look like they could contain asbestos, stop and get them tested. Hazardous materials are excluded from your price.
4. Check the right people are on site doing the right work, and inspect yesterday's work.
5. Take photos, and back up the phone the same day.
6. Enter every receipt in Actual Costs the day you get it.
7. Before you leave: site locked, tools away, hallway protection in place. Write the daily report: 5 minutes, every day.

**Say it:**
> Friday update: "Week 1 update, job 2026-001. Done: strip-out, floor repair (CO-01), plumbing rough-in, passed inspection today. Photos attached. Next week: walls and floor prep, then waterproofing on Friday. Decision needed from you: none. Money: invoice 2026-001-INV-02 sent today."

**File it:** Daily reports go in 07 Daily Reports. Photos go in `08 Photos/YYYY-MM-DD/`.

**What can go wrong:** The day there's an argument is the day you skipped the report. A sub works without PPE because you didn't check.

**Why:** Memory loses arguments. Records win them.

## 10. Demolition finds a rotten floor: CO-01

**What happens.** On day 2 the tub comes out. The floor under it is soft and black, rotted by years of that leak. This is an **unforeseen condition**: something you couldn't see when you priced the job. Your contract says it's extra (clause 8), and your exclusions named it.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |

## 16. Waterproofing, flood test and tile

**What happens.** The shower must hold water before tile covers it. This is the second big hold point.

| SOP | Forms | Sheets |

2. **Month 4:** she sends a photo of a crack along the corner where the shower wall meets the floor. Acknowledge it within 24 hours. Ask whether water is getting out. It isn't, so you attend within 3 working days. If it were leaking, you'd go the same day.
3. Find the cause. Grout was used in that corner instead of flexible sealant. That's workmanship, and it slipped past your walk in stage 16. It falls under the tile sub's 12-month warranty.
4. Give the tile sub written notice to fix it within 48 hours. He does, at no cost. You check it, photograph it, and log it in the callback log. If he had refused, you'd have it fixed and deduct the cost from the B$70.50 retention you still hold. That's what the retention is for.
5. Add it to your tile check in bold: "change-of-plane joints = sealant, not grout."
6. **Month 11:** walk the bathroom with her before the 12 months run out. All good. Offer a maintenance service agreement for her house, and her sister's rental.
7. **Month 12:** release the final retention. Check each sub's documents first (clause 4.2). Pay the plumber B$82.50 and the tile sub B$70.50, each against a signed final release.
8. Mark the job **Closed** in Leads & Quotes. The folder stays complete and is never deleted.

**Say it:**
> "Thank you for the photo, Mrs. Rolle. That corner should have flexible sealant, not grout. It's covered under our warranty. My tile setter will fix it by Thursday at no cost, and I'll check it myself."

**What can go wrong:** You argue about whose fault it is, or take three weeks to show up. A B$50 repair becomes a story told at church.

**Why:** A fast, free fix on a real defect makes a client for life. A slow one undoes the whole job.
